# Ports and adapters for opencargo

Design contract for: a hexagonal boundary in this tree, sized for one maintainer working with AI agents. Line references and counts are to `6c9747a`; re-grep before editing. Reads `multi-format-proxy.md` (1-5, 14), `policy-report.md`, `release-chain.md`, and the parallel designs `s3.md` (1-3, 13), `sso.md` (3, 13), `ha-options.md` (1, 2, 4), `pypi.md`, `maven.md`, `nuget.md`, `migration-importers.md`. `mcp-governance.md` **is** present and claims `018_mcp.sql` (5.1 allocates it `023`); an MCP surface is one more application-layer caller, which is all a boundary needs from it.

## 0. The verdict, before the detail

Persistence is the only leak worth a port, and it is a bad one: `src/db/mod.rs` is 1179 lines and 54 public free functions over a concrete `SqlitePool`; `db::` is called from 232 places outside it; `SqlitePool` appears 72 times and `sqlx::` 146 times outside `src/db/`; 96 of the tree's 170 `sqlx::query` call sites are **outside** the persistence module, concentrated in `telemetry/cleanup.rs` (20), `api/dashboard.rs` (20) and `registry/oci/manifests.rs` (11). Everything else the owner might want to "hexagonalise" is either already a port (`StorageBackend`, `UpstreamStrategy`, `Rule`, `Leaf`) or should stay exactly as it is (section 6.3).

**The dominant form of the leak is none of those counts.** It is `crate::db::f(&state.db, ..)`: 232 `db::` calls and 186 `&state.db` field accesses (225 `.db` accesses in total outside `src/db/`), and **43 files call `db::` without ever naming `SqlitePool`** — `api/promote.rs` 15, `api/users.rs` 13, `registry/cargo/publish.rs` 11, `api/webhooks.rs` 10, `api/repositories.rs` 9, `registry/go/publish.rs` 7. A ratchet built only on `sqlx::query` and `SqlitePool` is blind in exactly those 43 files, which is why section 4.2 counts `db::` and `.db` directly.

Two things that are *not* in that list are equally load-bearing, because they are where a naive version of this plan fails:
- **The resolver's context object is a second pool.** `Cx<'a> { state: &'a AppState, .. }` (`resolve.rs:25-29`) is the sole argument of `Leaf::hosted`/`Leaf::proxy`, `AppState.db` is a `SqlitePool` (`server.rs:42-43`), and `cx.state` is named 25 times in `src/` — 23 dereferences (22 in `src/registry/`, one in `policy/mod.rs:424`), 13 of them straight to `cx.state.db`, plus `resolve.rs:210` and `:214` inside `walk_members`; and **twice the whole `&AppState` is passed on** (`resolve.rs:175` `Upstream::for_member(cx.state, repo)`, `npm/leaves.rs:162` `search_in_repo(cx.state, ..)`), which a `cx\.state\.` pattern cannot see. Porting `db::` without rewiring `Cx` leaves the pool in the hot path with a clean-looking trait around it. Section 2.6 and step 7a close it; the ratchet counts it, unscoped.
- **The schema is not in Rust.** 49 SQLite-dialect fragments live in `src/db/migrations/*.sql` (`AUTOINCREMENT`, `CHECK(<col> IN (..))`, the FTS5 virtual table and its three triggers), against **22** in `src/**/*.rs` outside `src/db/` — SQLite date arithmetic (`datetime('now')`, `datetime('now','-N days')`, `strftime(`) plus `AUTOINCREMENT` and `INSERT OR `, concentrated in `telemetry/cleanup.rs` (10), and including the two `policy/store.rs` predicates section 1.5 calls load-bearing. `migrate()` is 14 `include_str!` + `sqlx::raw_sql` calls (`db/mod.rs:93-136`) with no version table and no per-dialect directory, and `kinds.rs:373-380` parses a `CHECK` constraint out of `001_initial.sql` inside a test. A plan that ports every Rust query and not the schema has not made Postgres reachable. Section 2.7 gives it a port; **step 8a ships it first, not last** — every other design in `designs-next/` adds a `.sql` file, and five of them claim the number `015` (5.1).

So this is not a rewrite into four crates. It is: **finish the two ports that are half-built, add fifteen persistence ports grouped by aggregate, rewire the resolver context, port the migrations, and make the compiler refuse the leak.** Measured cost of the alternative framing: a full `cargo check` of the lib with warm dependencies is 0.56 s (all-targets 0.93 s, 408 crates in `Cargo.lock`). Compile time is not a reason to split crates here; correctness of the boundary is, and section 4 gets it without the split until it is nearly free.

### 1.2 Application — one use case per protocol operation and admin action

`src/app/`. A use case is a struct holding only the ports it needs, with one `async fn run`. There are 77 `State(state): State<AppState>` handlers today, plus 42 routes registered in `server.rs` and 28 in the four `registry/*/routes.rs`. Not every handler needs its own struct: a use case exists where a handler does more than *parse, call one port, encode*. The set:

**Protocol reads** — `ResolvePackument`, `ResolveTarball`, `ResolveDistTags`, `ResolveCargoIndex`, `ResolveCrate`, `ResolveGoList/Latest/File`, `ResolveOciManifest/Blob/Tags`, `Search`; each is today a `Leaf` impl plus a handler (`npm/leaves.rs:21,64,115,158`, 13 `impl Leaf`). **Protocol writes** — `PublishNpmVersion`, `PublishCrate`, `PublishGoModule`, `PutOciManifest`, `StartOciUpload`/`AppendChunk`/`CompleteUpload`, `SetDistTag`, `Yank`. **Admin** — `CreateRepository`, `UpdateRepository`, `DeleteRepository`, `PromoteVersion`, `CreateUser`, `UpdateUser`, `DeleteUser`, `IssueToken`, `RevokeToken`, `SetPermission`, `CreateWebhook`, `ScanVersion`, `RunCleanup` (`cleanup.rs:58-62`), `ReportTotals` (`policy/mod.rs:387`). **Cross-cutting** — `Authenticate` (`auth/middleware.rs`), `AuthorizeRepoAction`.

The application owns: authorization checks, the order of port calls, what is atomic, what I/O happens *outside* a transaction (2.4), **which audience an event has** (2.5), and what is recorded. It owns nothing about HTTP.

**`src/app/` is a deliverable with a done-when, not a diagram.** An earlier draft named it here and then created it in no step, so the use cases above, the authorization ordering and the "what is atomic" ownership had nowhere to land. It is created by **step 4** (`src/app/webhooks.rs`, the pilot's one use case), extended by **step 7** (one module per format commit, carrying that format's publish/resolve ordering and its authorization — the shared post-publish tail `registry/publish.rs::finalize_publish` is explicitly *not* in it; 2.5 says why and gives it to 8e) and closed by **step 8** (the admin and background tail). A use case never calls another; handlers construct them and stay parse-call-encode. Anything still not in `src/app/` when step 8 closes is struck from the list above at that review rather than left as an aspiration.

### 1.4 What stays outside the domain because it is protocol detail

Named explicitly, because the temptation runs the other way: npm's `dist-tags` JSON shape and `_attachments` base64 (`npm/publish.rs:28-46`); the cargo index's one-crate-per-line NDJSON and its `cksum` field; the `Docker-Upload-UUID` and `Location` headers of a chunked push (`oci/uploads.rs:96-101`); OCI's `WWW-Authenticate` challenge and bearer scope strings (`oci/token.rs:191`); go's `!`-escaping (`go/escape.rs`); PyPI's Simple HTML and Maven's `maven-metadata.xml`. ### 1.5 The domain types *are* the SQLite encoding today, and it already reaches the wire

Dropping `#[derive(FromRow)]` moves a type; it does not make it dialect-free. `db/mod.rs:15-26,38-51` is `Repository { visibility: String, config_json: Option<String>, created_at: String, updated_at: String }` and `Version { published_at: String, yanked: i64 }` — every one of those is a SQLite storage format, not a registry concept. The proof that this is not cosmetic is `registry/go/mod.rs:12-21`: `rfc3339()` parses `published_at` with `"%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"`, names SQLite's `datetime('now')` in its own doc comment, and on a parse failure does `unwrap_or_else(|_| published_at.to_string())` — it **echoes the raw column into the go `Time` field**. A Postgres adapter returning `2026-09-17 12:00:00+00` therefore emits a non-RFC-3339 `@v/list` and `.info` with no error logged anywhere, and section 4.2's dialect row cannot see it because `go/mod.rs:12` is a comment.

So **step 3 retypes at the adapter boundary**, it does not merely move:

| field | today | domain type | retyped in |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Repository.visibility` | `String` | `Visibility` | step 3 |
| `Repository.config` | `Option<String>` (`config_json`) | **`Option<RepoConfig>`** — `None` ≠ `default()`; tolerant, see below | step 3 |
| `Repository`/`Package`/`Version` `created_at`/`updated_at`/`published_at` | `String` | `DateTime<Utc>` | step 3 |
| `Version.yanked` | `i64` | `bool` | step 3 |
| `ApiToken.{expires_at,last_used_at,created_at}`, `User.{created_at,updated_at}` | `String` | `DateTime<Utc>` | step 6 |
| `Webhook.{created_at,updated_at}`, `AuditEntry.created_at` | `String` | `DateTime<Utc>` | step 8c |

**The retype may not stop at the three protocol row types, because the one it would skip is the auth path and it fails closed.** `db/mod.rs:436-447`'s `ApiToken.expires_at` is written `"%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"` (`api/tokens.rs:96`, `server.rs:592`) and read back by `auth/middleware.rs:458-465`, whose own comment is *"Fail closed: an unparseable timestamp counts as expired."* Leave it a `String` and step 6 puts it in `TokenStore`'s signature unchanged, so the port's contract silently becomes *a SQLite-formatted string*: a second adapter returning RFC 3339 or a `timestamptz` expires **every** token that has an expiry, as 401s, and `store_contract!`'s round-trip clause cannot catch it because that clause only covers `DateTime<Utc>` fields. So step 6 retypes those three columns and **deletes `expired()`'s string parse for a `DateTime<Utc>` comparison**. `User.*` (`api/users.rs:60,61,133,155,156,234,235`), `Webhook.*` (`api/webhooks.rs:50-51`) and `AuditEntry.created_at` (`api/audit.rs:61`, `api/promote.rs:401`) are the same leak at lower stakes and ride step 8c, each under the same red-then-green + `wire_ts` rule as step 3. The row → domain conversion is the *only* place a text timestamp is parsed, and a failure there is `DomainError::CorruptColumn`, never a silent fallback. `rfc3339()` and its `unwrap_or_else` are **deleted** in the same commit (its three call sites format a `DateTime<Utc>` directly). **`config_json` is the one column that must stay tolerant.** `parse_group_members` (`db/mod.rs:406-416`) is *totally* forgiving — bad JSON, a missing `members` key and non-string entries all yield an empty list, i.e. "not a group" — so a strict `RepoConfig::from_json` turns a repository with a garbage column into a `CorruptColumn` on **every** `by_name`, including the admin call that would fix it. It moves into `RepoConfig::from_json` keeping exactly that tolerance: unparseable ⇒ `RepoConfig::default()` plus one `warn!`, with `CorruptColumn` reserved for `repo_type`, `format` and the timestamps. **And `NULL` is not a garbage value, it is the majority case.** `kinds.rs:120-125`'s `config_json()` writes the column *only* for a group, so every hosted and every proxy repository has it `NULL` and `repositories.rs:297` emits `"config": null` for all of them. Retyping the field to a bare `RepoConfig` and pairing it with `wire_config(&RepoConfig)` flips that field to `"{\"members\":[]}"` on every non-group repository — and **neither assertion cited below can see it**: `group_resolver_test.rs:373,380` only assert `g5`, which *is* a group, and `types.ts:50`'s `config: string | null` still typechecks. So the domain field is `Option<RepoConfig>` and `wire_config` is `Option<&RepoConfig> -> Option<String>`: `None` round-trips to `NULL` and to JSON `null`, and `Some(RepoConfig::default())` — a group with an empty member list — is a different value from `None`. `RepoConfig` also carries `#[serde(flatten)] extra: serde_json::Map` so a round-trip through `update` (`api/repositories.rs:161`) cannot drop a key the struct does not model.

**Retyping changes six wire surfaces; two have no test, and one has a test that forbids the change.** `go/mod.rs:12-21` at least *parses*; two more sites echo the raw column straight out. `api/repositories.rs:298-299` puts `repo.created_at`/`updated_at` into the JSON of `GET /api/v1/repositories`, and `npm/packument.rs:32-39` puts `package.created_at`, `package.updated_at` and every `v.published_at` into the packument's `time` map. Retyping those fields to `DateTime<Utc>` compiles and flips both from `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS` to RFC 3339 with **nothing going red**: no test asserts the hosted packument's `time` (the only `"time"` assertions in `tests/` are against the fake upstream, `tests/common/fake_upstream/npm.rs:73` and `policy_test.rs:67-74`). Meanwhile `api/dashboard.rs`'s raw tuple queries keep emitting the old format (`:258-261`, `:456`, `:515-518`) until step 8c, so `frontend/src/core/types.ts`'s `created_at`/`published_at` consumers would see both formats at once for weeks. The fifth is not a timestamp and is the one that goes red: `api/repositories.rs:297` emits `"config": repo.config_json`, a JSON **string**, asserted byte-for-byte at `tests/group_resolver_test.rs:373,380` (`r#"{"members":["h","p6"]}"#`) — for `g5`, a *group*, the only shape those two assertions reach — and typed `config: string | null` at `frontend/src/core/types.ts:50` — and rule 7.5 #2 forbids editing that test. A bare retype flips the field to an object and breaks both. The sixth is `"visibility"` (`api/me.rs:38,87`, `api/repositories.rs:295`), and it is benign only by accident: `config::Visibility` (`config.rs:239-245`) derives `Deserialize` but **not** `Serialize`, so the retype fails to compile rather than flipping the bytes silently — step 3 gives it `Serialize` + `#[serde(rename_all = "lowercase")]`, which four live assertions pin. So step 3:

- adds `fn wire_ts(DateTime<Utc>) -> String` (RFC 3339) **and `fn wire_config(Option<&RepoConfig>) -> Option<String>`** in the HTTP adapter, the mirrors of the adapter's `bind_ts` and `RepoConfig::from_json`; serde over `{ members: Vec<String>, #[serde(flatten)] extra }` reproduces the asserted bytes exactly and `None` stays JSON `null`, so `group_resolver_test.rs` stays green **unmodified** and `types.ts` needs no edit;
- lands **three new assertions first, in their own commit** — the hosted packument's `time` values are RFC 3339, `GET /api/v1/repositories`'s `created_at` is RFC 3339, and `GET /api/v1/repositories/{hosted}` has `"config": null`. The first two go red on the commit that retypes, then green: a visible red-then-green. The third is green on both sides by construction and is the regression guard — it is the only assertion in the tree that would catch `Option` being flattened away;
- applies `wire_ts` in the *same* commit at `repositories.rs:298-299`, `packument.rs:32-39`, `npm/search.rs:100` (`"date": v.published_at.as_str()`, an unlisted fifth site), `go/mod.rs`'s three call sites **and** the dashboard's raw-tuple panels, which `bind_ts`-parse on the way out until step 8c retypes them properly; and `wire_config` at `repositories.rs:297`, with `repositories.rs:161`'s write path building a `RepoConfig` instead of a `json!` string. No surface ever serves two formats.

**The stored representation is part of the SQLite adapter's contract, and the round-trip assertion is to the second.** An earlier draft asserted a *bit-identical* round-trip; this schema cannot satisfy it and must not try. 23 columns are written by `DEFAULT (datetime('now'))` (`001_initial.sql:9,10,20,21,35,50`, `003`, `006`, `008`, `009`, `010`, `013`, `014`) in second-precision `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`, and two live predicates compare those strings **lexicographically**: `proxy_cache.rs:33` `expires_at > datetime('now')` and `policy/store.rs:316` `created_at < datetime('now','-N days')`. An adapter preserving sub-second precision would have to write RFC 3339, and `'T'` (0x54) sorts above `' '` (0x20), so every legacy and every DEFAULT-written row would compare wrong against a new-format value — cache freshness mis-evaluated on the artifact-serving hot path, policy retention deleting everything or nothing. So:

- The SQLite adapter's stored timestamp is **UTC, second precision, `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`**, produced by one `fn bind_ts(DateTime<Utc>) -> String` used by every statement in the adapter. It exists already, three times: `policy/store.rs:152-154`'s `sqlite_time`, and `expiry.format("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")` at `api/tokens.rs:96` and `server.rs:592` (read back by `auth/middleware.rs:461`). Step 5 makes it the adapter's single one.
- **The 23 `DEFAULT (datetime('now'))` columns stay.** An earlier draft dropped them "as each port lands". SQLite's `ALTER TABLE` cannot drop a default, so every one of them is the 12-step table rebuild under `foreign_keys=ON` — and rebuilding `packages` drops the three triggers `007_fts5.sql:8-20` declares on it together with the external-content link, which 8a has already recorded as applied so it never re-runs: search stops indexing new publishes with no error at all, which is precisely the failure the deleted `LIKE` fallback used to mask. No step owns that work, no id is allocated for it in 5.1, and it is not in the sizing. The defaults are inert once every adapter statement binds `bind_ts(now)` — which is exactly why **`now: DateTime<Utc>` is a parameter of every store method that writes a timestamp column** (2.2), without which the clause below cannot be written for 14 of the 15 stores. The property actually wanted is a `store_contract!` clause: **a row inserted through any store carries the timestamp the caller passed, never the server clock**, which a fake clock in the use case makes assertable and which fails loudly if an adapter ever lets a DEFAULT fire. A schema rebuild, if one is ever needed, is its own migration and recreates `007`'s triggers in the same file.
- `store_contract!` (7.3) asserts *a `DateTime<Utc>` written through the store and read back is equal to the value written **after truncating both sides** to whole seconds* — a tolerance, not a demand that the adapter be lossy. `read.trunc_subsecs(0) == written.trunc_subsecs(0)` passes for SQLite, which truncates, and for a Postgres `timestamptz` or a `FakeDb` that keeps full precision; the earlier `read == written.trunc_subsecs(0)` form would have failed both for being *correct*. That still makes the `go/mod.rs:12-21` regression impossible, because that one is a format bug, not a precision bug. `policy_resolutions.published_at` is already RFC 3339 (`policy/store.rs:31-33`) and stays so: it is a data column, never compared against `datetime('now')`. Full sub-second precision everywhere else needs a migration converting all timestamp columns *and* both predicates — its own step, not a contract line.

## 2. The ports

### 2.1 Already ports; leave them alone

- **`Rule`** (`policy/rules/mod.rs:69-78`) — pure, synchronous, takes `now: DateTime<Utc>` as a parameter, four impls, `Vec<Box<dyn Rule>>` at `:82`. This is the model the rest should imitate. No change.
- **`UpstreamStrategy`** (`proxy/strategy.rs:58-118`) — per-format upstream behaviour as pure functions of an associated `Artifact`, 4 real impls + 1 fixture. Keep, with two repairs and one deliberate non-repair. `upstream_url` returns `reqwest::Url` (`:61`) — move to `url::Url`, which is in the lock file via reqwest but **not** a direct dependency, so step 7a adds the `url` line to `Cargo.toml` (and the domain allowlist of 4.2/4.3 is unaffected, because `url` lives in `src/ports/`, not `src/domain/`). **And it is no more error-clean than `StorageBackend` is:** `upstream_url` returns `AppResult<reqwest::Url>` and `verify_headers` `AppResult<()>` (`:61, 77-81`), so the port a doc comment calls "a pure function of the artifact" hands back a type carrying `sqlx::Error` and a `StatusCode`. Both move onto `ResolveError` in step 7a, with `Leaf` (below). The non-repair: `verify_headers` **keeps** `http::HeaderMap` — `axum::http` is a re-export of the `http` crate, section 3 has already ruled that a port at the edge may name `tokio::io::AsyncRead`, `verify_headers` runs per upstream response on the artifact path, and a newtype there buys a copy per response, no second implementation and no test seam.
- **`StorageBackend`** (`storage/mod.rs:13-37`) — a real port with a designed second implementation, **but not yet used as one**: `AppState.storage` is `Arc<FilesystemStorage>` (`server.rs:44`), `ProxyEngine.storage` is `Arc<FilesystemStorage>` (`engine/mod.rs:53`), and `PartFile::{new, commit}` take `&FilesystemStorage` to call the concrete `resolve()` (`engine/payload.rs:161, 184`; `filesystem.rs:28`). Only `telemetry/cleanup.rs:10` holds `Arc<dyn StorageBackend>`. Closing this is step 2a; `s3.md` decision 2 in full is 2b. **It is also not error-clean:** all eleven methods return `Result<_, AppError>` (`storage/mod.rs:14-36`), and `AppError` carries `Database(sqlx::Error)` and axum's `StatusCode` (`error.rs:11-50`) — the defect section 3 diagnoses for `Repository::{kind,fmt}`, in a trait the application is about to call directly. Step 2a touches every method signature anyway, so it also introduces `StorageError { NotFound, InvalidPath(String), Unavailable, Other }` with `From<StorageError> for AppError` mapping `NotFound` -> 404 and **`InvalidPath` -> 400**, and adds `crate::error` to the deny-list scope for `src/storage/` and `src/adapters/fs/`. **`InvalidPath` is not a fourth variant for symmetry — without it 2a silently changes a status.** `filesystem.rs` constructs exactly two `AppError`s: `NotFound` twice (`:113,192`) and **`BadRequest` seven times** (`:37,45,48,66,88,94,96`), every one of them inside `safe_path`, the traversal / invalid-key guard. A three-variant enum has no home for it, so every rejected key would collapse into `Other` -> 500 and `filesystem.rs:280`'s `is_bad_request` assertion would go red inside the commit that is supposed to change nothing. It belongs in the port contract rather than in an opaque `Other` for a second reason: `s3.md`'s `validate_key` is the same guard over a different key space, so "this key is not addressable" is a fact every backend has and must report identically. `resolve()`, kept on the trait until 2b, returns `Result<PathBuf, StorageError>` like the rest.

**`Leaf` is the exception: the trait shape is right and its argument is wrong.** `Leaf` (`resolve.rs:78-88`, 13 impls) stays generic with its associated `Out`, and `first_hit<L: Leaf>` (`:97`) stays generic. But its only argument is `&Cx<'_>`, and `Cx` holds `&AppState`, so today every leaf can and does reach the pool. Section 2.6 replaces `Cx`'s contents without touching the trait's signature — that is the whole point of the newtype: the method list does not move, the thing behind the pointer does. **Its error type moves, though, and it has to: `Leaf`'s `AppError` carries domain semantics.** `Leaf::{hosted,proxy}` return `AppResult<Outcome<Self::Out>>` (`resolve.rs:80-87`), and the degraded/miss vocabulary section 1.1 calls *domain* is encoded in `AppError` variants — `Walk::record` (`:143-146`) matches `Err(AppError::NotFound(_))` to mean *this member had nothing*, and `Walk::miss` (`:155-158`) **constructs** `AppError::BadGateway`/`NotFound` to mean *the group degraded* / *nothing anywhere*. So step 7a retypes `Leaf`, `Walk`, `Upstream::for_member` and `UpstreamStrategy` onto `pub enum ResolveError { NotFound, Upstream(String), Domain(DomainError), Store(StoreError), Internal(String) }`. **The last two variants are not padding — without them the retype does not compile and two live statuses are lost.** `walk_members` swallows only `Unauthorized|Forbidden` (`resolve.rs:214-217`); everything else it propagates with `?`, and that is two things: `crate::db::get_repository_by_name` at `:210` (today `AppError::Database`, 500) and `ensure_can_read`'s other error, which is `permissions.rs:39-44`'s deliberate `ServiceUnavailable` → **503**, written there precisely so a failing database cannot silently fall back to role defaults. After 7a both are `StoreError`, and folding them into `Upstream(_)` (502) or `Domain(_)` (4xx) would silently change both. So `From<StoreError> for ResolveError` yields `Store(e)`, and `From<ResolveError> for AppError` maps `Store(Unavailable)` → 503, every other `Store` → 500, `Internal` → 500, `NotFound` → 404, `Upstream` → 502, `Domain` through the existing `From<DomainError>`. **That mapping alone still loses the 503, because `permissions.rs:36-44` is deliberately blanket.** It maps *every* error out of `get_user_permission` to `ServiceUnavailable` — its doc comment says why: applying the role default while the database is failing would stop enforcing an explicit deny, so the caller gets a retryable 503 — whereas §2.4 produces `StoreError::Unavailable` only for `SQLITE_BUSY`/`BUSY_SNAPSHOT` after the retry. A disk-I/O error, a corrupt page or a closed pool during the grant lookup would therefore fall into "every other `Store`" and become a 500, turning a fail-retryable authorization path into a fail-opaque one. So the rule is stated at the authorization path, not at the mapping table: **any `StoreError` out of the grant lookup collapses to `ResolveError::Store(StoreError::Unavailable)`** — today's blanket, spelled once, in the one place where "the store is unreliable" and "authorization is unsafe to decide" are the same fact. Everywhere else the `Unavailable`/`Other` distinction is kept. `Internal(String)` carries the two cases that are configuration faults rather than domain ones and keeps their 500: `MAX_GROUP_DEPTH` (`:196`, `AppError::Internal` today — `DomainError` gets no `GroupDepthExceeded` variant, because a depth cap reached at *read* time means a row that write-time validation should have refused) and `Upstream::for_member`'s missing or unparseable `upstream_url` (`:47,53`), which is a config error and not `CorruptColumn`. `resolve.rs:371-415`'s `matches!(.., Err(AppError::…))` assertions become `ResolveError` ones in that commit, and no HTTP status changes.

**One rule governs every signature below: a store method that writes a timestamp column — or whose predicate compares a column against the clock — takes `now: DateTime<Utc>`** — as a parameter, or as a field of its command struct when it already has one. **The predicate half is not symmetry: it is where the dialect actually is.** A write-only rule leaves `PolicyStore::delete_older_than(days)` (`policy/store.rs:317`, `created_at < datetime('now','-' || ?1 || ' days')`) and `ProxyCacheStore::evictable(idle)` (`proxy_cache.rs:126-127`) reading the database's clock inside the adapter — the same SQLite date arithmetic 1.5 shows is lexicographic and therefore dialect-bound, in the one place a Postgres adapter cannot copy the string. So the two retention methods are `delete_older_than(days, now)` and `evictable(idle, now, limit)`, and after them every clock comparison in a predicate is a bound parameter. This is also what makes 1.5's contract clause (*a row inserted through any store carries the timestamp the caller passed, never the server clock*) writable at all, and it is why 2.8 can say the clock is mostly not a port. An earlier draft showed `now` only on `ProxyCacheStore` and left it off `RepositoryStore`, at which point 14 of the 15 stores had to read a clock inside the adapter or let a column DEFAULT fire, the contract clause degenerated to *the adapter's own clock round-trips*, and the 23 `DEFAULT (datetime('now'))` columns 1.5 leaves in place stopped being inert. Both exemplars therefore carry it, so the next feature copies the right shape (`now` fills `created_at`/`updated_at`; the `DEFAULT (datetime('now'))` at `001_initial.sql:9,10` is never allowed to fire):

### 2.3 `SearchIndex` is one read method, and `007_fts5.sql` stops being optional

`packages_fts` appears at exactly two call sites in Rust (`api/dashboard.rs:581-590`, `npm/search.rs:118-131`) because **nothing in Rust writes it**: `007_fts5.sql:8-20` declares three triggers (`AFTER INSERT`/`DELETE`/`UPDATE ON packages`) that maintain it invisibly, and `migrate()` swallows that file's error entirely (`db/mod.rs:116-118`: *"FTS5 might not be available on all SQLite builds; ignore the error"*).

An earlier draft answered this by giving the port a write side — `index()`/`remove()` called inside `publish_version`, a documented no-op in the SQLite adapter, pinned by a contract assertion that *search reflects a publish*. **That does not work in this tree, and the reason is in the two call sites.** Both already degrade to `LIKE`: `npm/search.rs:129-140` is `if let Ok(packages) = fts { return Ok(packages); }` followed by an unconditional `LIKE` query, and `api/dashboard.rs:594` is an `Err(_) => {` fallback arm. A missing `packages_fts` therefore does not make search "quietly return nothing" — it silently returns LIKE results, so the contract assertion passes through the fallback even when `007` was skipped and `index()` is a no-op. `index`/`remove` would then have exactly one real implementation, the fake, which is the ceremony section 6.3 refuses everywhere else. **Decision: fix the migration instead of decorating the port.** `sqlx`'s `sqlite` feature bundles SQLite with FTS5 compiled in (`Cargo.toml:26`), so for a single static binary FTS5 availability is fixed at build time and the `let _ =` is superstition, not tolerance.

- Step 8a applies `007` **strictly**, like every other file. If FTS5 is genuinely absent the process fails at boot with the reason, instead of degrading for the rest of its life.
- **`007` has no backfill, and without one a strict apply builds an *empty* index.** The whole file is `CREATE VIRTUAL TABLE IF NOT EXISTS packages_fts … content=packages` plus the three triggers: no `INSERT INTO packages_fts(packages_fts) VALUES('rebuild')`, no seed select (`rebuild` has zero hits anywhere in the migrations directory), and the triggers fire only on *future* writes to `packages`. So on any install where `packages_fts` is absent while `packages` is populated — exactly the case 2.7's probe-and-adopt handles by *applying* `007` — the index comes up empty, and deleting both fallbacks then makes npm search and the dashboard return zero hits for every pre-existing package, silently and forever. That is a second behavioural delta of the deletion and it is worse than the `?q=%20` one below; an earlier draft enumerated only the first. So **8a appends `INSERT INTO packages_fts(packages_fts) VALUES('rebuild');` to `007`'s `Step`** — idempotent and cheap — **and allocates `015_fts_rebuild.sql`, whose only content is that same rebuild**, for the installs that *adopt* `007` rather than apply it. Baseline test (c) of 2.7 gains the clause: a `012` fixture with rows in `packages` must have a **non-empty** `packages_fts` after the run.
- The two `LIKE` fallbacks are deleted in step 7's npm commit and step 8c. An `Err` from the FTS query is a `StoreError`, not a silent downgrade. **But deleting them is not behaviour-neutral, and two inputs prove it — and one of them is the default.** Both sites build `fts_query` by `split_whitespace()`-ing the user's text and quoting each token (`dashboard.rs:575-579`, `search.rs:115-119`); both gate only on *non-empty*, not on *has a token*. So `?q=%20` produces `MATCH ''`, which real FTS5 rejects (`fts5: syntax error near ""`) — today that falls through to `LIKE`, and with the fallback gone it is a 500. A lone `"` (`?q=%22`) is the same family one level down — non-empty, one token, and that token sanitises away, so the phrase handed to FTS5 is `""`; the rule below folds it into the same arm rather than leaving the implementer to discover what FTS5 does with an empty phrase. Every other input they build is accepted, quoting included (`"left-pad"`, `"@scope/pkg"`, `"-"`). **The second input is no input at all.** `search.rs:107` gates FTS on `if !text.is_empty()`, `:23` types `text: Option<String>` and `:42` is `unwrap_or_default()`, so `GET /{repo}/-/v1/search?size=20` — npm's *browse*, the call a client makes with no query — falls straight to `LIKE '%%'`, which returns every package in the repository up to `limit`. A rule that says "an empty token list means the use case returns `Ok(vec![])`" would therefore replace *all packages* with *none* on the endpoint's default path, and no existing assertion can see it: the four search tests (`npm_test.rs:224,281,292`, `e2e_scoped_test.rs:405`, `npm_proxy_test.rs:375,611`) all pass a non-empty `text`, so rule 7.5 #2 passes while the endpoint regresses. (The dashboard has no such path: `dashboard.rs:631` already returns `Vec::new()` for an empty `q`.) So **an empty query is a browse, not a no-op**, and the distinction lives in the HTTP adapter, which is where it lives today: absent or empty `text` ⇒ `None` ⇒ browse; a non-empty `text` that tokenises to nothing ⇒ `Ok(vec![])` without ever calling the port, which is what `?q=%20`'s `LIKE '% %'` already amounts to.
- `SearchIndex` is **one method**. `suggest` has zero call sites in this tree and is deleted from the design — it is exactly the ceremony 6.3 refuses; add it when a caller exists. Each adapter maintains its own index inside its own `publish_version`/`delete` — SQLite's triggers already do, and a Postgres adapter writes its `tsvector` in the same statement. The index is an adapter's private business; what the application depends on is that a search after a publish finds the package, which `cascade_contract!` asserts against every adapter (7.3).

**The signature must fit both call sites, and `search(repo, ..)` fits neither.** `api/dashboard.rs:581-590` searches **across all repositories** with a visibility predicate spliced into the SQL (`visibility_sql`, `:177-189`) and returns `(id, name, description)`; there is no `RepoId` to pass. `npm/search.rs:118-131` returns full `Package` rows ordered by `rank`, so a `Vec<PackageId>` return forces an N+1 rehydrate *and* throws away the ordering the port's own contract clause (7.3) is about. Hence:

```rust
// src/ports/search.rs — dashboard.rs:177-189 has exactly two arms (admin ⇒ no predicate, everyone else
// ⇒ public-only), which `Visible(Visibility)` cannot express without `Visible(Private)` secretly meaning

pub enum SearchScope { Repo(RepoId), PublicOnly, All }
#[async_trait] pub trait SearchIndex: Send + Sync {
    /// `Some(q)`: relevance-ordered. `None`: a **browse** — the scope's packages in the adapter's natural
```

### 2.4 The transaction boundary: transactional use cases, not a unit of work

Three candidates, and this tree settles it.
1. **Executor passing** (`&mut dyn Executor<'_, Database = Sqlite>` through every method) — puts `sqlx` in the port signature: the leak, spelled with extra ceremony. Refused.
2. **Generic unit of work** (`trait Uow { type Tx<'a>; async fn begin(&self) -> Self::Tx<'_> }`) — the associated type makes every store non-object-safe, so `AppState` can no longer hold `Arc<dyn ...>` and every use case grows `where S: RepositoryStore` bounds that propagate into 77 handlers. For one maintainer, the wrong trade.
3. **Coarse port methods that *are* the transaction.** Chosen. `error.rs:69-71` already states the problem in the tree's own words: *"Full atomicity via DB transactions around publish/promote remains a follow-up — it needs threading a `&mut Transaction` through the DAL."* Option 3 is the version that never threads one: the port method names a business operation, the adapter opens `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` around it, and the dialect stays where the dialect lives.

Two rules keep option 3 from degenerating. **Rule A — coarse only for atomicity.** A port method is coarse only when two or more writes must be atomic. Everything else is one statement and needs no boundary.

**Rule B — no storage, network or upstream I/O inside a store method.** A store method may touch only the database. This is not purism: `db/mod.rs:78-84` builds a 5-connection pool in WAL mode with a 5 s `busy_timeout`, and SQLite has one writer. `api/promote.rs:206-219` does `state.storage.get(&from)` then `state.storage.put(&target)` — a full in-memory copy of an artifact bounded by `MAX_BODY_BYTES` = 1 GiB (`server.rs:39`) — between its reads and its writes. A `promote` port method that owned `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` around that would hold the single writer for the length of a gigabyte copy; every concurrent publish would exhaust the 5 s timeout, return `SQLITE_BUSY` → `AppError::Database` → 500. Once `s3.md` lands, the same span becomes a network round-trip inside the write lock.

So the eight coarse methods are:

| use case | writes | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `PackageStore::publish_version` | package upsert + version + dist-tags (+ whatever the adapter's own index needs, 2.3) | `npm/publish.rs:106-121` (4 sequential awaits, no transaction) |
| `PackageStore::promote_metadata` | **package upsert in the target repository** + version row + **its dist-tags** + audit row (metadata only) | `api/promote.rs:173-192, 195-247` |
| `PackageStore::delete_version` | `dist_tags` + `downloads` + `download_counts` + version row | `telemetry/cleanup.rs:146-166`, 4 statements, no transaction, no cascade |
| `RepositoryStore::delete_empty` | conflict check + repo row + grants + `proxy_cache_meta`/`_entries` | `db/mod.rs:926`, `api/repositories.rs:196-218` |
| `OciStore::put_manifest` | manifest + blobs + tags | `oci/manifests.rs`, 11 queries |
| `OciStore::delete_manifest` -> `Orphaned { manifest_path, blob_digests }` | `oci_tags` + `oci_manifests` + `oci_manifest_blobs`, **and the `oci_blobs` rows the link deletion orphaned**; returns the keys, deletes no file | `oci/manifests.rs:227-320`, `gc_orphaned_blobs` `:287` |
| `UserStore::delete` | user + tokens + grants (**not** policy rows — see below) | `db/mod.rs:537` |
| `PolicyStore::insert_batch` | already batched | `policy/store.rs:10` |

**`delete_version` and `delete_manifest` are the two deletion paths that actually cascade today, and an earlier draft had no owner for either.** It said deep deletion was out of scope because `db/mod.rs` declares no `delete_package`/`delete_version` — true of `db/mod.rs`, and irrelevant, because the deletions live outside it. `telemetry/cleanup.rs:146-166` removes a version across four tables with no transaction and no `ON DELETE CASCADE` on any of them (`001_initial.sql:41,43,49`); `oci/manifests.rs:227-320` removes a manifest across four more (`006_oci.sql:3,13,24,34`, none cascading). Without ports for them, step 8d's `telemetry/cleanup.rs (20)` row has nowhere to move its queries and cannot reach 0. **Rule B applies to both, but not in the same direction, and an earlier draft said "storage first, rows second" for both.** That is right for `delete_version` — the version row carries `tarball_path`, so `cleanup.rs:130-143`'s order is safe and step 8d keeps it. It is wrong for `delete_manifest`: `gc_orphaned_blobs` (`:287-320`) interleaves the `oci_manifest_blobs`/`oci_blobs` deletes with `storage.delete(blob_path(..))`, and its orphan test (`COUNT(*) … WHERE blob_digest = ?`) is only meaningful *after* the link rows are gone — the orphan set does not exist until the transaction has run. Deleting files first and then failing the commit leaves a live manifest whose layers are gone: a corrupt registry, worse than today's untransacted rows-first order. So `delete_manifest` inverts it: the store transaction does the rows and **returns what it orphaned**, the `DeleteOciManifest` use case deletes those objects from storage **after** the commit, and a crash between the two leaves unreferenced files — a leak, not a collectable state (see `PromoteVersion` below), so the use case logs the `Orphaned` set at `warn!` **before** the storage pass, which is the only record anything has of what was meant to go. Rule B is satisfied either way — no storage call happens inside a store method.

**The transaction must be `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, not sqlx's default.** `Pool::begin` emits a bare `"BEGIN"` (`sqlx-core-0.8.6/src/transaction.rs:277-281`, passed through by `sqlx-sqlite-0.8.6/src/transaction.rs:15-30`), which is SQLite's **deferred** shape, and every coarse method above is read-then-write: `publish_version` looks the package up then inserts, `delete_empty` runs the conflict check then deletes, `promote_metadata` reads the source version then inserts, `put_manifest` reads then writes. In WAL, a deferred transaction that reads and *then* tries to upgrade after another connection committed gets `SQLITE_BUSY_SNAPSHOT` **immediately** — the 5 s `busy_timeout` at `db/mod.rs:79` is not consulted for that case, because the snapshot is already stale and waiting cannot fix it. Today nothing hits this: every write is its own implicit transaction. This design would introduce it on the publish path while claiming to fix the concurrency story, turning concurrent npm publishes into `StoreError::Other` → 500 — replacing the 409 it just deleted. So:

- every coarse method opens `pool.begin_with("BEGIN IMMEDIATE")` (`sqlx-core-0.8.6/src/pool/mod.rs:391`), taking the write lock up front, where `busy_timeout` *does* apply;
- the SQLite adapter wraps that in a bounded retry — 3 attempts, jittered backoff — on `SQLITE_BUSY`/`SQLITE_BUSY_SNAPSHOT`, and only then maps to `StoreError::Unavailable` (503), never `Other` (500);
- `store_contract!` (7.3) gains a case that drives two concurrent `publish_version` calls of the same `package@version` and asserts **exactly one `Conflict` and zero `Other`**. It is the only assertion that can tell the two shapes apart, and it runs against every adapter.

`PromoteVersion` is therefore a use case with the I/O *outside* the transaction, in this order: (1) read the source version and resolve the target path; (2) `storage.put(target, bytes)`, idempotent on the target path — writing the same bytes twice to the same key is a no-op in both the FS and the S3 adapter; (3) `PackageStore::promote_metadata(..)`, a DB-only transaction carrying **the package upsert in the target repository** (`promote.rs:173-192` creates it when absent and re-reads it, today outside any boundary — left out of the coarse method it is a fourth state "all three or none" cannot see: an empty package row in the target repo, which the `007` triggers have already indexed), the version row, **each dist-tag the source version holds** (`promote.rs:242-247` copies them one `set_dist_tag` at a time today, outside any boundary — a half-failed promote leaves a version visible with no `latest` tag, a state no GC collects) and the audit row. If (3) fails, an orphan blob exists at the target path with no version row pointing at it. **There is no GC that collects it, and an earlier draft claimed there was.** `promote.rs:206-210` says a different thing — paths are per-version so that deleting one version's file cannot destroy another version's artifact — and `telemetry/cleanup.rs` holds only `cleanup_old_prereleases` (deletes the tarballs of versions it is deleting), `sweep_proxy_cache` (deletes the file of the cache row it is evicting) and `remove_stale_parts` (scratch `*.part-*`). Nothing enumerates storage and deletes what no row claims, and nothing can: `StorageBackend` (`storage/mod.rs:13-37`) has no `list`; that method arrives only with `s3.md`'s 2b, which is **not in this plan's budget**. So the design does not lean on a sweeper it does not have. Instead: **(i)** `PromoteVersion` compensates — a failure at (3) is followed by a best-effort `storage.delete(target)`, which is safe because (2) is idempotent on that key and the key belongs to a version that does not exist; a crash between (2) and (3) is the residual window; **(ii)** `DeleteOciManifest` logs its `Orphaned` set before the storage pass; **(iii)** the design states plainly that orphan *reclamation* — a sweep that lists storage and drops what no row references — is blocked on `StorageBackend::list`, i.e. on 2b, and until then has no owner. `cascade_contract!` asserts the leak as the outcome of an injected failure (7.3) because that is what the tree does, not because it is collected.

**Cascades are contract, and almost nothing cascades for free.** An earlier draft said the SQLite adapter got its cascades from the schema. It does not. `ON DELETE CASCADE` is declared on exactly six foreign keys — `003_auth.sql:13` (tokens), `008_deps.sql:3,4`, `009_vulns.sql:3`, `010_dynamic_config.sql:4,5` (grants), `013_proxy_cache_entries.sql:3`, `014_policy.sql:20`. It is **absent** from `packages.repository_id` (`001:15`), `versions.package_id` (`001:27`), `dist_tags` (`001:41,43`), `downloads` (`001:49`), `proxy_cache_meta` (`002:3`), all four `006_oci.sql` tables (`:3,13,24,34`), `012:9`, and `audit_log.user_id` (`003:25`). With `foreign_keys=ON` (`db/mod.rs:80-81`), deleting a repository that still has packages does not cascade — it **fails** with a FK violation. Which is why the product refuses it outright (`api/repositories.rs:196-205`, asserted by `tests/permissions_test.rs:595-603`, a test rule 7.5 #2 forbids editing). A deep *repository* cascade would be new deletion behaviour and a product change, not a port move; it is out of scope here. The *version* and *manifest* cascades are not — they exist today, hand-rolled and untransacted, and `PackageStore::delete_version` / `OciStore::delete_manifest` are where they land.

So the cascade lists above are **normative parts of the port contract**, and every entry is something the code performs today:
- `delete_empty` returns `Conflict` when packages or group memberships remain, cascades grants (`010`) and `proxy_cache_entries` (`013`) through the schema, and deletes `proxy_cache_meta` explicitly because `002:3` has no cascade. The proxy blob purge (`api/repositories.rs:215-217`) stays *outside* the transaction, under rule B.
- `UserStore::delete` takes tokens (free, `003:13`) and grants (free, `010:4`) into the transaction, and leaves `audit_log.user_id` pointing at the deleted id exactly as today — `003:25` has no cascade and the audit trail is meant to outlive the user. Changing that is a migration, not a port method.
- **Policy erasure is deliberately not in that transaction.** `policy::store::delete_by_user` (`policy/store.rs:322-335`) chunks at `DELETE_CHUNK = 5000` with a yield between transactions, and says why in its own comment: *"the write lock is held for one chunk, never for a whole day of resolutions and their verdicts."* Folding it into one coarse `UserStore::delete` reintroduces exactly the single-writer stall rule B exists to prevent. It stays a separate `PolicyStore::erase_user(user_id)` — the eighth `PolicyStore` method — called by the `DeleteUser` use case after the store transaction commits, keeping its chunking.

Hand-written fakes do none of this unless told to, and a per-port contract suite cannot catch the divergence: a test that deletes a repo through `FakeRepositoryStore` and then reads `FakePermissionStore` sees stale grants and nothing fails. Section 7 answers that with a single `FakeDb` and a `cascade_contract!` suite.

The immediate win beyond hexagonal purity: `error.rs:64-80` currently sniffs `sqlx::Error::Database(_).is_unique_violation()` at the **HTTP layer** to turn a concurrent double publish into a 409. After this, `publish_version` returns `StoreError::Conflict` and the sniff dies with it — **but not until the last format is ported.** That sniff is the only 409 for the concurrent double-publish race on *every* format (the sequential case is caught by explicit pre-checks such as `promote.rs:195-203`), and step 7 is four commits over two to three weeks. Deleting it with the npm commit turns the race into a 500 for cargo, go and OCI in between. It is deleted in step 7's **last** commit (OCI), and the step row says so.

### 2.4bis The serializable inventory, and the restore epoch beside it

Two facts of the contract, not of an adapter (frozen HA design, invariants I6, I7, I10 and checklist C-1 to C-13).

**Serializable execution.** A method must run under a serializable execution when it spends a `PinToken` by compare-and-set, or writes a table `ReferencedKeys` reads — `versions`, `proxy_cache_entries`, `oci_blobs`, `oci_manifests`, `oci_uploads`, `maven_files`, `pypi_files`. Both sides of the pin/claim race need the same isolation: a serializable reclamation against a `READ COMMITTED` write proves nothing. SQLite has it from `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` on every coarse method (2.4); another dialect owes it an option of its own, `SERIALIZABLE` with a bounded retry or a lock on the logical key, and a transient failure there is `StoreError::Unavailable`, never `Other`. The inventory is `ports::reclaim::SERIALIZABLE`, and each adapter carries a test that fails when one of its functions writes one of those tables and is not mapped onto a listed port method.

**The restore epoch and the high-water mark.** The database persists an installation identifier, an opaque restore epoch — drawn at random, never a counter, because two branches of one backup would increment it to the same value — and the counter of the mark it last wrote. Every generation is minted under the epoch and names it, so `reusable()` never returns a generation from the other side of a restore. The mark (installation, epoch, counter) lives in the artifact store under `_opencargo/`, a reserved tree excluded from every scan and from `storage verify`, and is compared both ways at startup and at every sweep: a database behind the mark draws a fresh epoch, a store behind the database owes a verify, another installation's mark is a hard refusal, and an unreadable one refuses reclamation and nothing else. The mark is written before the counter it advances, so a crash between the two leaves only the conservative case. Until the verify it owes has run, reclamation deletes nothing; that verify reports the rows whose object is gone, queues what a rollback orphaned under a live incarnation, and with `--repair` puts back the last noncurrent version of a missing key on a store that keeps them.

### 2.5 Events, scanner, signer, upstream

`// src/ports/events.rs` — the bus is already an object (`events.rs:44`), so the trait over it is one line: `pub trait Events: Send + Sync { fn emit(&self, event: DomainEvent, to: Audience); }`, never failing and never blocking (`events.rs:74-75`). `DomainEvent` is a typed enum, not `serde_json::Value` (`events.rs:36`). The JSON shape is the WebSocket adapter's job (`api/ws.rs`); the audience is not, and an earlier draft that handed the adapter the `Visibility` fan-out (`events.rs:22-27`) is refuted by `registry/mod.rs:340-363`. `emit_package_event` **reads the repository row** to choose `Visibility::Public` or `::Admin`, and in the private branch emits a *second*, anonymized `registry.changed` at `Authenticated`; `api/ws.rs:90` then filters on `ev.visibility <= identity.level` and on nothing else. A WS adapter with no store would have to default, and defaulting `package.published` to `Public` publishes private package names to anonymous subscribers — a disclosure, introduced by a refactor that was meant to be behaviour-preserving. So `Audience` (the domain spelling of `events.rs:22-27`) is decided by the application, where the `Repository` is already in hand; the private-repo two-event fan-out stays a decision of the `Publish*` use cases; the adapter only filters and encodes.

**`registry/publish.rs::finalize_publish` is where that decision physically lives today, and it needs an owner before step 7 claims it.** All four formats end their publish in `finalize_publish(state: &AppState, ..)` (`npm/publish.rs:287`, `cargo/publish.rs:100`, `go/publish.rs:76`, `oci/manifests.rs:130`), which dispatches the webhook, calls `emit_package_event` — the `Audience` decision above — and spawns the OSV scan on the pool. Three of those are ports that do not exist at step 7 (`Events`, `VulnFeed`, `VulnStore` land at 8b and 8e), and 6.3 forbids handing a use case `&AppState` to bridge the gap. **Decision: the tail is not pulled forward.** `finalize_publish` stays exactly as it is — a handler-level function taking `&AppState`, called after the use case returns — until **8e** moves it onto `Events` + `Audience`, at which point it becomes a `PublishTail` collaborator the four `Publish*` use cases hold by its three ports. Step 7's done-when therefore claims that format's *ordering and authorization*, not its event audience, and 8e's files column carries `registry/publish.rs`. Why a port: tests assert emissions without a WS client, and `ha-options.md` 1.4 names the in-process broadcast as the thing that breaks visibly under two instances — a `LISTEN`/`NOTIFY` or Redis adapter has somewhere to go.

```rust
// src/ports/vulns.rs
#[async_trait] pub trait VulnFeed: Send + Sync { fn enabled(&self) -> bool;
    async fn assess_batch(&self, eco: &str, deps: &[(String, String)]) -> Result<Vec<Vec<VulnDetail>>, ScanError>; }
```
This is `VulnScanner::{enabled, assess_batch}` (`vulns/mod.rs:84, 90`) minus the persistence: `persist` (`:136`) and `scan_version` (`:164`) take a `&SqlitePool` and call `crate::db::insert_vulnerability_scan`, so today the scanner is a scanner *and* a repository. `scan_version` becomes the `ScanVersion` use case over `VulnFeed` + `VulnStore` — and note it is a rule-B case too: the OSV round-trip happens before the `VulnStore` write, never inside it.

`// src/ports/tokens.rs` — `pub trait RegistryTokenSigner: Send + Sync { fn sign(&self, c: &Claims) -> String; fn verify(&self, t: &str) -> Option<Claims>; }`. `TokenSigner` (`oci/token.rs:55-74`) is already exactly this shape, built as `TokenSigner::random()` once per process at `server.rs:93`. `ha-options.md` 1.3 calls the per-process key a correctness blocker; the port is where its section 5 persisted-key implementation lands, and it also lets `oci_auth_test.rs` assert rejection with a deterministic key.

**Upstream fetcher: no new trait.** `UpstreamStrategy` (2.1) is the per-format *policy* and stays; the transport is `reqwest::Client` inside `ProxyEngine` (`engine/mod.rs:52, 90-95`) and must stay concrete. Reason in section 6.3.

### 2.6 `Cx`: the resolver context stops carrying the pool

This is the bypass the rest of the plan would otherwise leave open, and it is worth its own subsection because nothing in clippy or a `db::` grep can see it: `cx.state.db` is not a `db::` call and not a `SqlitePool` *mention*, it is a field access.

Today (`resolve.rs:25-29, 45-74`) it is `Cx<'a> { state: &'a AppState, auth: Option<&'a AuthUser>, url: UrlRepo<'a> }` with `Upstream::for_member(state: &AppState, member: &Repository)`. After (step 7a):
```rust
pub struct Cx<'a> { pub repos: &'a dyn RepositoryStore, pub perms: &'a dyn PermissionStore,
    pub packages: &'a dyn PackageStore, pub oci: &'a dyn OciStore, pub search: &'a dyn SearchIndex,
    pub proxy: &'a ProxyEngine, pub policy: &'a dyn ResolutionRecorder, pub creds: &'a dyn UpstreamCredsSource,
    pub auth: Option<&'a AuthUser>, pub url: UrlRepo<'a>, pub base_url: &'a str }
impl Upstream { pub fn for_member(creds: &dyn UpstreamCredsSource, m: &Repository) -> Result<Self, ResolveError> { .. } }
```

**`proxy` stays a concrete `ProxyEngine`, and that is only sound because step 5 empties it first.** `ProxyEngine` (`engine/mod.rs:49-58`) is a concrete struct whose private fields are `db: SqlitePool` and `storage: Arc<FilesystemStorage>`, and 8 of the 25 `cx.state` sites are `cx.state.proxy` (`npm/leaves.rs:41,97`, `go/leaves.rs:43`, `cargo/leaves.rs:53,89`, `oci/leaves.rs:23,41,211`). A `Cx` whose `proxy` field still carried a pool would drive the `cx.state` row to 0 while every proxy leaf reached the database one field over — the exact bypass this subsection exists to close — and `resolve.rs:313-346`'s fixture could not collapse into map literals either, because constructing a `ProxyEngine` would still demand a temp dir and a pool even though `Script` never touches `cx.proxy`. The `.db` and `SqlitePool` rows of 4.2 do see the 14 `self.db` sites behind that field, but only 8e's blanket zero *forces* them — three steps after 7a has certified the boundary. So **step 5 owns it**, not 8e: `db: SqlitePool` becomes `cache: Arc<dyn ProxyCacheStore>`, and with 2a having done the storage half `ProxyEngine::new(Arc<dyn StorageBackend>, Arc<dyn ProxyCacheStore>, Timeouts, TtlConfig)` is constructible from fakes. **`sqlx`/`SqlitePool` = 0 in `src/proxy/` is therefore a precondition of 7a**, written into step 5's done-when. No trait is introduced for the engine itself (6.3): it stays a concrete collaborator that now holds only ports.
**`oci` and `search` are what make the row reach 0, and without them 7a does not compile.** Four of the 25 sites want neither `repos` nor `packages`: `oci/leaves.rs:60` (`db::oci::get_blob`), `:109` (`resolve_hosted_digest` + `db::oci::get_manifest`) and `:193` (a raw `SELECT tag FROM oci_tags … .fetch_all(&cx.state.db)`) need `OciStore`; `npm/leaves.rs:162` (`search_in_repo(cx.state, ..)`) needs `SearchIndex`. `SearchIndex` arrives with step 7's npm commit, which precedes 7a, so it is already in hand. **`OciStore` is not, and 7a therefore builds its read half itself.** An earlier draft left `OciStore` entirely to step 7's oci commit — which 7a precedes and which depends on 7a — so 7a could not compile from the files its own row listed, and the ordering was circular. 7a ships `src/ports/oci.rs` with `blob`, `manifest`, `digest_for_ref` and `tags` — exactly the four reads those three sites make — plus `src/adapters/sqlite/oci.rs`; step 7's oci commit **extends** that trait with `put_manifest`/`delete_manifest` and their `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transactions (2.4), and both step rows say so. This also fixes an ordering defect in step 7: `oci/leaves.rs:193` sits on the `Leaf` path and can only lose its query once the leaf holds a store, so **7a lands immediately after step 7's npm commit and step 7's oci commit depends on it** — otherwise the oci commit's "0 `sqlx` in that format's modules" either precedes its own precondition or builds a throwaway store out of `cx.state.db` that 7a then rewrites. Both step rows say so. **The `policy` field is not optional.** `policy/mod.rs:424` (`let engine = &cx.state.policy;`) is the twenty-third `cx.state.` site, it is `record_resolution` on the leaf path, and it cannot be lifted to the handler — so without a field for it the `cx.state` row of 4.2 bottoms out at 1 and the step is unfinishable. `ResolutionRecorder { fn records(&self, repo: &str) -> bool; fn record(&self, p: Pending); }` is the two methods `policy/mod.rs:424-430` actually calls, `PolicyEngine` (`server.rs:59`) is its one real impl, and a fake makes the "a degraded group resolution is recorded" assertion of §7 possible without a `PolicyEngine`.
**`creds` is why `for_member` cannot simply take a `&UpstreamCreds`.** `Upstream::for_member` has **one** call site, not five (`resolve.rs:175`), and it is *inside* `walk` — on a member the HTTP adapter never sees, because `walk_members` (`:210`) discovers it mid-recursion. The creds come from `state.upstream_auth: Arc<HashMap<String, UpstreamCreds>>` (`server.rs:48`) keyed by **member name** (`resolve.rs:59-61`), so no single resolved `&UpstreamCreds` can be passed down from the handler; a `Cx` without a lookup leaves `cx.state` in `walk` and the `cx.state` ratchet row bottoms out above zero. Hence a field of its own and a one-method port — `trait UpstreamCredsSource { fn for_repo(&self, name: &str) -> UpstreamCreds; }`, whose one real impl is the existing `Arc<HashMap<String, UpstreamCreds>>` and whose fake is a map literal. (The trait cannot be called `UpstreamCreds`: that name is taken by the struct at `proxy/auth.rs:48`.)

`Cx` is built once by the HTTP adapter per request, exactly where `AppState` already lives. `walk_members`'s two calls (`resolve.rs:210` `crate::db::get_repository_by_name(&cx.state.db, ..)`, `:214` `ensure_can_read(&cx.state.db, ..)`) become `cx.repos.by_name(name)` and a pure `permissions::can_read(grant, cx.auth)` over a `cx.perms` lookup. The `Leaf` trait signature does not change; the 13 impls change only where they say `cx.state.db`.

**Section 3's rule stands and is not in tension with this:** `Cx` must not be a *port argument* — no store method ever takes a `Cx`. `Cx` is the application's request context, and after this step what it contains is ports, which is what makes `resolve.rs:313-346`'s `build_state`-plus-temp-DB fixture collapse into map literals. That collapse is delivered by step 7a and by nothing else.

### 2.7 The schema: one migrator, per-dialect directories

`migrate()` (`db/mod.rs:93-136`) is 14 hard-coded `include_str!` + `sqlx::raw_sql` pairs, three of which deliberately ignore their error (`004`, `005`, `007`), with no version table: re-running it is the versioning scheme. 49 dialect fragments live in those files. This is the actual Postgres blocker and the previous draft of this design did not count it, because its ratchet scope was `src/**/*.rs`.

**No `Migrator` trait — not yet, and this is the one place the design declines a port it earlier promised.** `applied`, `apply` and `baseline` are each irreducibly dialect-specific (`sqlite_master`, `PRAGMA table_info`, SQLite's `IF NOT EXISTS` semantics), the postgres side is an empty directory with a README, and all three baseline tests below run against SQLite. A trait with one implementation, no second implementation and no test seam is exactly the ceremony 6.3 refuses everywhere else, and 6.3's own definition would reject it. So 8a ships a plain adapter module:

```rust
// src/adapters/sqlite/migrate.rs — version table, probe and apply loop in one function. `StoreError` is
// step 1's, which is why step 1 precedes 8a (5.1).
pub enum Step { Sql(&'static str), Rust(fn(&mut SqliteConnection) -> BoxFuture<'_, Result<(), StoreError>>) }
pub async fn run(pool: &SqlitePool, steps: &[(MigrationId, Step)]) -> Result<(), StoreError>;
```
The trait arrives with the commit that adds the second dialect — the only commit that can say what belongs in it.

**`Step` is not generality for its own sake: a `&'static str`-only signature admits none of the three Rust migrations already designed.** `nuget.md:375-376` and `maven.md:341-343` specify `rebuild_repositories_check` as Rust *inside* `migrate()` — one **acquired** connection, `PRAGMA foreign_keys=OFF` (per connection, `db/mod.rs:79-81`), `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, the `sqlite_sequence` restore, an orphan-set diff that rolls back and bails, `PRAGMA foreign_keys=ON` — and `mcp-governance.md:10,147` requires the same rebuild to admit `mcp` into `repositories.format`'s `CHECK`. None of that is a file. Under a file-only migrator it either lives outside the version table, re-running on every boot behind a `sqlite_master`-text guard the tree then carries forever, or the connection that ran it returns to a 5-connection pool with FK enforcement **off** — and one request in five silently skips the `003:13`, `010:4` and `013:3` cascades that 2.4 and `cascade_contract!` are built on. So: the runner **acquires one connection per step** and calls `.detach()` on it, rather than returning it to the pool, for every `Rust` step and on every error path.

**The baseline is still not optional, and getting it wrong bricks production.** Twelve of the fourteen files are `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` / `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS` and re-run harmlessly. The two that are not are `004_cargo.sql` (`ALTER TABLE versions ADD COLUMN yanked …`) and `005_must_change_password.sql` (`ALTER TABLE users ADD COLUMN must_change_password …`) — bare `ALTER TABLE`, no `IF NOT EXISTS` clause available in SQLite, which is exactly why `db/mod.rs:104-112` wraps them in `let _ =`. On a database that exists today — including the one on the OVH cluster running `0.1.0-rc.1` — a strict migrator that has just created an empty `schema_migrations` sees `applied() == []`, re-runs `004`, gets `duplicate column name: yanked`, and **the process dies on boot**. Turning the two swallows into "explicit already applied" without a baseline converts a harmless no-op into an outage. **And blind baselining bricks a partially-migrated database.** An earlier draft's rule was: `schema_migrations` absent **and** `repositories` present ⇒ record `001`-`014` as applied, execute nothing. That is only safe if every pre-migrator database ran all fourteen files. They have not, and the counter-example is in this repository: `data/db/opencargo.db` has `repositories`, `user_permissions` (`010`) and `oci_manifest_blobs` (`012`) but **no `proxy_cache_entries` (`013`), no `policy_resolutions`/`policy_verdicts` (`014`)** — the file predates those two and today's `migrate()` creates them on the next boot, because all fourteen run unconditionally and twelve of them are `IF NOT EXISTS`. Blind baselining records `013` and `014` as applied, never creates their tables, and every proxy-cache lookup and every policy write fails at runtime, forever. Worse, the test that was supposed to guard it builds its fixture *with today's `migrate()`* — a fully migrated file by construction — so it passes while production breaks.

So the baseline **probes**, it does not assume — and each id's sentinel is the object its file creates **last**, never its first. A first-object sentinel is a trap: `sqlx::raw_sql` runs a file's statements with no enclosing transaction, so a partially-applied file leaves its early objects present and its tail missing, which is harmless today *only* because all fourteen files re-run on every boot under `IF NOT EXISTS`, and becomes permanent and silent the moment 8a applies each file at most once. Five of the fourteen create more than one object (`001` five tables + four indexes, `003` three tables, `006` four tables, `010` two tables, `014` two tables + six indexes), so for those five the choice is the whole guarantee. The last object is the one that exists only if the entire file ran. Every sentinel is checkable with `SELECT 1 FROM sqlite_master WHERE name=?` — indexes and triggers are rows there too — except the two `ALTER TABLE`s, which need `PRAGMA table_info`: `001` `idx_downloads_version`, `002` `proxy_cache_meta`, `003` `idx_audit_log_created`, `004` column `versions.yanked`, `005` column `users.must_change_password`, `006` `oci_uploads`, `007` trigger `packages_fts_update`, `008` `idx_package_deps_version`, `009` `idx_vuln_scans_version`, `010` `webhooks`, `011` `download_counts`, `012` `idx_oci_manifest_blobs_blob`, `013` `idx_proxy_cache_entries_last_used`, `014` `idx_policy_verdicts_res`.

**And a shipped file is immutable: a change to one is a new id, never an edit — which is the opposite of this tree's current habit.** `014_policy.sql` has been edited in place *after* it shipped: its tail is `DROP INDEX IF EXISTS idx_policy_res_created` plus `idx_policy_res_created_flags` and `idx_policy_verdicts_res`, added later to reach databases that had already run the earlier `014`, and it works only because today every file re-runs every boot. **`014`'s own past edit needs no repair, and understanding why is what fixes the rule.** The sentinel is chosen at 8a from the file's *current* last object — `idx_policy_verdicts_res`, one of the statements the in-place edit added. A database that ran the old `014` completely therefore **fails** the probe, so 8a *applies* `014` rather than adopting it, and its `DROP INDEX IF EXISTS idx_policy_res_created` and three `IF NOT EXISTS` index creations all run: the edit reaches exactly the installs it was meant to. No `016_policy_index_fix.sql` is needed and none is allocated. The danger is the *next* edit — one made after 8a has frozen the sentinel list, whose new tail object no longer matches the recorded sentinel, or worse whose change is invisible to it — and that is precisely what the checksum row catches rather than the migrator. So the rule set is: a shipped file may not be renamed, deleted **or modified**; `scripts/boundary.sh` pins each file by **checksum**, not only by name (5.1), so an in-place edit fails CI with the fix named in the message, and the only legal way to change a shipped file's effect is the next free id. `007` is the case that genuinely needs a companion file, and it gets one (`015_fts_rebuild.sql`, 2.3) because its gap is a *missing backfill* on installs that legitimately adopt it, not an edit.

`run(migrator, dir)` is then, in order:

1. `schema_migrations` absent and `repositories` absent ⇒ fresh database: create `schema_migrations`, apply everything from `001`, strictly.
2. `schema_migrations` absent and `repositories` present ⇒ the database predates the migrator: create `schema_migrations`, then **probe and adopt** — in id order, record every id whose sentinel already exists and **apply** every id whose sentinel does not. (Not one transaction: the probe is idempotent, so a crash mid-adopt is simply re-probed on the next boot, which is a stronger guarantee than a cross-file DDL transaction and costs nothing.) `data/db/opencargo.db` adopts `001`-`012` and executes `013` and `014`; a fully migrated file adopts all fourteen and executes nothing; and the two `ALTER TABLE`s only ever run when their column is genuinely missing, which is what makes `let _ =` unnecessary rather than merely unfashionable.
3. Otherwise apply, strictly and in id order, every file not in `applied()`. `007_fts5.sql` is applied strictly like the rest — section 2.3. The adopt list is always explicitly probed, never a range literal.

Deliverables, all in **step 8a** (see 5.1): `git mv src/db/migrations src/adapters/sqlite/migrations`; `007`'s rebuild line and `015_fts_rebuild.sql` (2.3); `schema_migrations(id TEXT PRIMARY KEY, applied_at TEXT NOT NULL)`; the probe-and-adopt above; an `src/adapters/postgres/migrations/` directory that exists and is empty with a README saying so; and `kinds.rs:373-380`'s `check_values()` — which greps `CHECK(<col> IN (..))` out of `001_initial.sql` in a test — replaced by the enum's own `ALL` slice, with the SQLite adapter asserting agreement in *its* test rather than the domain asserting it against a dialect.

**The files stay inside the crate, and that is deliberate.** `Dockerfile:12-18` copies `Cargo.toml`, `Cargo.lock`, `src/` and the frontend dist and nothing else; the image is the deployment artifact the release chain promotes by digest. A `git mv` to a top-level `migrations/` puts the `.sql` files outside the build context and the image fails at `include_str!` — a release-blocking break for a directory rename. `src/adapters/sqlite/migrations/` gives the same per-dialect separation, keeps `include_str!` working, keeps `scripts/release/build-musl.sh` (which mounts `$PWD`) working, and leaves the Dockerfile untouched. Four tests pin the baseline, and step 8a is not done without them: **(a)** build a database with today's `migrate()`, copy the file, run the new migrator against the copy — it must succeed, adopt `001`-`014`, execute only `015`, leave `yanked`/`must_change_password` single-columned, and end with **the same `sqlite_master` object set as a fresh database**, which is the property that makes adoption safe at all and the one an in-place edit of a shipped file breaks; **(b)** run the new migrator twice against a fresh file — the second run must execute nothing; **(c)** the one the earlier draft lacked: a fixture at `012`, built by running only `001`-`012` (the schema of `data/db/opencargo.db`, dumped and committed as a `.sql` fixture, not copied from a live cluster), against which the migrator must leave `proxy_cache_entries` and `policy_resolutions` **existing** — and, with rows seeded into `packages` before the run, `packages_fts` **non-empty** (2.3); **(d)** a `Rust` step that runs the `nuget.md`/`maven.md` rebuild against a fresh file — afterwards `PRAGMA foreign_keys` reads `ON` on all five pooled connections, and it reads `ON` again after the same step is forced to fail mid-transaction. Step 8a's done-when also includes `docker build .` passing. **Ids are keys, so a shipped file may never be renumbered — nor edited.** Renaming `015_a.sql` to `016_a.sql` after deployment makes installs that already ran `015_a` re-run it as `016_a` — a second strict `ALTER TABLE` and the same boot failure; editing it in place is the quieter twin, reaching only the installs that had not yet run it. The number allocation in 5.1 exists to make the first never necessary and the checksum row to make the second impossible.

### 2.8 Clock, ids, randomness — three answers, only one of them a trait

- **Clock: mostly not a port.** `Rule::evaluate` already takes `now: DateTime<Utc>` (`rules/mod.rs:76`), which is the right shape and needs nothing. Of **36** wall-clock sites (`Utc::now()` 33, `SystemTime::now()` 3 — an earlier draft said 59, which counted the 23 `Instant::now()` calls as clocks), the ones that matter are not in Rust at all: **the database is the clock**, including the two constants the whole proxy cache turns on (`proxy_cache.rs:33` `FRESH`, `:36` `EXPIRY`). Pass `now` into the store — which 2.2 makes a rule for every timestamp-writing method, not a courtesy of `ProxyCacheStore` — and that disappears. The residual port is one method, for the background loops that sleep:
  `pub trait Clock: Send + Sync { fn now(&self) -> DateTime<Utc>; }`, used by `RunCleanup` (`cleanup.rs:58-62`, the loop and its 24 h `tokio::time::sleep`; `:22-28` is `struct CleanupStats`, not code) and token expiry. Not used by anything that can take `now` as an argument instead — and **explicitly not the policy pacer**, which an earlier draft named here. `policy/pacer.rs` makes zero wall-clock calls: its `cooldown_until: Mutex<Option<Instant>>` (`:27`, compared `:36`, set `:40`) is a deliberately *monotonic* deadline that must not follow a wall clock across an NTP step. **`Instant::now()` is never ported**, here or anywhere.
- **Ids: a two-method port.** `Uuid::new_v4()` appears at `api/tokens.rs:89`, `server.rs:585`, `oci/uploads.rs:62` (values that appear in responses and in assertions) and at `filesystem.rs:233`, `engine/transfer.rs:113` (scratch-file suffixes that never leave the adapter). Port only the first three: `trait Ids { fn token_id(&self) -> String; fn upload_id(&self) -> String; }`. The scratch suffixes stay where they are — porting them is how a boundary starts to look like ceremony.
- **Randomness: no port.** `auth/tokens.rs:8` generates a token, `users.rs` generates a password, `TokenSigner::random()` a key. Each is a *secret*, and a fake that makes secrets predictable is a footgun that will eventually be reachable from a non-test build. Determinism where it is needed comes from constructing the adapter with a fixed seed (`TokenSigner::from_key(&[u8; 32])`), not from a trait.

## 3. Rust mechanics

**Async in traits.** Rust 1.93 has `async fn` in traits and RPITIT, but an `async fn` in a trait is **not object-safe**, and `async fn` gives no `Send` guarantee on the returned future — which `tokio::spawn` needs (`policy/mod.rs:319`, `main.rs:83`). So the rule splits cleanly by dispatch:

- Ports that must be `dyn` (every store, `StorageBackend`, `VulnFeed`, `Events`, `Clock`, `Ids`): `#[async_trait]`. It is already a dependency (`Cargo.toml:47`) and already used for exactly this (`storage/mod.rs:13`). Cost is one `Box::pin` per call, ~20 ns against 10-100 µs of I/O.
- Ports that are generic because they carry an associated type (`Leaf` with `type Out`, `UpstreamStrategy` with `type Artifact`): RPITIT with an explicit `Send`, so they allocate nothing and the pure hooks inline:
  ```rust
  pub trait Leaf: Send + Sync { type Out: Send;  // after 7a the error is ResolveError, not AppError (2.1)
      fn hosted(&self, cx: &Cx<'_>, m: CacheRepo<'_>) -> impl Future<Output = Result<Outcome<Self::Out>, ResolveError>> + Send; }
  ```
  One caveat specific to this tree: `walk` is mutually recursive and already returns `Pin<Box<dyn Future + Send>>` (`resolve.rs:163-170`), so the resolver keeps one box per group level whatever `Leaf` does. Converting `Leaf` off `async_trait` is therefore a small, optional win — do it alongside step 7a or never. Note that `Cx` now holds `&dyn` store references, so a `Cx<'_>` argument is still one pointer-pair per port and costs nothing extra.

**Which ports are `dyn`, which are generic.** Rule: *generic iff the trait has an associated type and performs no I/O; `dyn` otherwise.* That gives `dyn` for all 15 stores and the 6 infrastructure ports, generic for `Leaf` (13 impls) and `UpstreamStrategy` (5 impls). The monomorphization cost is already paid and visible: `ProxyEngine::{run, lookup, settle, exchange, head, peek, resolve_row}` × 5 strategies, `first_hit`/`collect`/`walk`/`walk_members` × 13 leaves. With `opt-level = "z"`, `lto = true`, `codegen-units = 1` (`Cargo.toml:107-110`) that is a size trade the release profile is already making deliberately; do not extend it to the stores.

**Hot path, quantified.** The proxy engine and the resolver serve every artifact, so the number matters. A cache hit (`engine/mod.rs:154-172`) costs: one SHA-256 of the cache key (`payload.rs:142`), one `get_entry` (`proxy_cache.rs:38-56`: pool checkout, prepared statement, SQLite page reads), a `resolve_row`, and a `read_stream` opening a file. The DB call alone is 10-100 µs; the file open is 10-50 µs. A monomorphic `dyn` call is an indirect branch through a vtable slot the branch-target buffer predicts perfectly in this shape: **1-3 ns worst case, effectively 0 amortised** — four to five orders of magnitude below the operation it dispatches to. Making the stores `dyn` is free on the hot path. Where dynamic dispatch would actually cost is the pure per-artifact hooks called several times per request (`cache_key`, `cache_policy`, `classify_status`, `max_bytes`), and those are precisely the ones staying generic and inlinable in `UpstreamStrategy`. There is no measurement that justifies generic stores; if one ever appears, it will be a `Vec<Repository>` allocation in `walk_members` (`resolve.rs:200-226`), not a vtable.

**Lifetimes.** Ports borrow in and own out: `&str`, `&[u8]`, `&NewEntry<'_>` as arguments; `Repository`, `Vec<CacheEntry>`, `String` as returns. Never return a borrow from a store — the adapter's data is the connection's, and `async_trait`'s desugaring cannot express the lifetime anyway. **`Cx<'a>` is never a port argument**: it is the application's request context, and after section 2.6 it holds `&dyn` ports rather than `&AppState`. Use cases take `&self` (their ports) plus a small owned or borrowed command struct. `CacheRepo<'a>(&'a Repository)` (`resolve.rs:23`) crosses freely — it borrows a domain value the caller already owns, which is the distinction that matters.

**Errors, three layers.**

```rust
// domain: no dependencies at all
pub enum DomainError { NotFound(Resource), Conflict(Resource), Forbidden(Action), InvalidName(String),
    FormatMismatch { want: Format, got: Format },  // no GroupDepthExceeded: it is ResolveError::Internal, 2.1
    CorruptColumn { repo: String, column: &'static str, value: String } }
// ports: what any store can fail with, dialect-free
pub enum StoreError { NotFound, Conflict, Unavailable, Other(Box<dyn Error + Send + Sync>) }
impl From<DomainError> for AppError { .. }  impl From<StoreError> for AppError { .. }  // adapter: behaviour unchanged
```
`AppError` (`error.rs:11-50`) keeps `IntoResponse` (`:52`) and its exact status mapping, and loses two variants: `Database(sqlx::Error)` (`:43`) and the unique-violation sniff (`:64-80`), which becomes `StoreError::Conflict` -> 409 in one line. `ServiceUnavailable` (`:34`) stays and gains a principled source: `StoreError::Unavailable`, which is what `permissions.rs:39-44` already hand-rolls. The ordering constraint: `AppError::Database` must survive until the *last* store moves (step 8), because 100+ `?` operators depend on the `From<sqlx::Error>` today.

`CorruptColumn` is in that enum for a specific reason. `Repository::{kind, fmt}` (`kinds.rs:331-341`) return `AppResult` and construct an `AppError::Internal` via `corrupt_column` (`:346-350`), and `AppError` is defined in a module whose first line is `use axum::{http::StatusCode, ..}` and which wraps `sqlx::Error`. So these two methods — which section 1.1 moves into `src/domain/` — drag axum and sqlx into the domain through a type whose *name* contains neither token. **This is why step 1 (errors) must land before step 3 (type moves)**, and why the domain-purity check is a deny-list on `use crate::…` rather than a grep for three crate names (section 4).

**Keeping sqlx, axum, reqwest and tokio out of the domain.** `sqlx` leaves via the row-struct split (`RepositoryRow` with `FromRow` in the adapter, `impl From<RepositoryRow> for Repository`). `axum` leaves via `DomainError`, and via `ResolveError` on `Leaf`/`Walk`/`UpstreamStrategy` (2.1). It does **not** leave `verify_headers`: `strategy.rs:77` spells `HeaderMap` through `axum::http`, which is a re-export of the `http` crate, and that is vocabulary at the edge on exactly the `AsyncRead` reasoning below. `reqwest` leaves via `url::Url` (`strategy.rs:61`, `resolve.rs:39`). `tokio` is subtler: `StorageBackend::read_stream` returns `Pin<Box<dyn AsyncRead + Send>>` (`storage/mod.rs:29-32`) — `AsyncRead` is `tokio::io`, and that is **fine**, because `StorageBackend` is a port *at the edge*, not a domain type; the domain never names a stream. `chrono`, `bytes` and `serde` are allowed in the domain: they are vocabulary crates, not infrastructure.

## 4. Enforcement: one crate, compiler-checked, with the split held in reserve

**Chosen: module discipline inside the single crate, enforced by a ratchet script from day 0 plus `clippy.toml` entries phased in as each ratchet reaches zero — and the domain promoted to its own crate as the *last* migration step, not the first.**

Why not a four-crate workspace now. The only thing a split adds over the check below is compiler-enforced acyclicity, and it costs a long-running restructuring branch, which is the named risk of this whole exercise (section 6.2). It buys nothing on compile time: the entire lib checks in 0.56 s warm. And it front-loads the orphan-rule work — with `Repository` in `opencargo-domain` and `FromRow` in `sqlx`, the adapter crate cannot `impl FromRow for Repository` and needs the row-struct split *immediately*, across all four types, in one commit. Step 3 does the same work incrementally.

### 4.1 `clippy.toml`, and why it cannot ship on day 0

CI runs `cargo clippy --all-targets --all-features -- -D warnings` (`.github/workflows/ci.yml:64`). `disallowed_types` and `disallowed_methods` fire **per use site**, so committing the full list before the code moves turns CI red on the first commit: **170** `sqlx::query` sites in `src/` (clippy sees all of them, `src/db` included — unlike the ratchet row of 4.2, which is scoped), 23 more in `tests/` (which `--all-targets` compiles), **148** `SqlitePool`, 77 `State<AppState>` bindings. "Guardrail first, no code moves" and "the full `clippy.toml` in step 0" are incompatible, and the guardrail is the part worth keeping.

Worse, the silencing mechanism is **per lint, not per path**. `#![allow(clippy::disallowed_types)]` at the top of a module legalises *every* disallowed type in that file. If `axum::extract::State` is on the list, every HTTP module needs that allow to keep its extractors — and the same allow then legalises `SqlitePool` in the same file. `api/dashboard.rs`, with 20 raw queries and the single worst leak in the tree, would be permanently exempt from the type check **by construction**. The check would be structurally unable to fire where the violations are. Both problems have the same fix: put on the list only what the code no longer does, and only paths whose per-file allow means one unambiguous thing.

```toml
# clippy.toml — the END state, assembled one entry at a time (see the "added in" column). Every entry
# carries a `reason`; SqlitePool is an *alias* for Pool<Sqlite> and clippy matches the RESOLVED path, so
# list every spelling — one entry short is a bypass — and keep the greps of 4.2 alongside. A path's first
# segment is a CRATE NAME (`crate` is not special-cased), so a local type is spelled `opencargo::…`.
disallowed-types = [   # step 8e, reason = "persistence adapter only: src/adapters/sqlite"
  "sqlx::SqlitePool", "sqlx::Pool", "sqlx::Sqlite", "sqlx::SqliteConnection", "sqlx::Transaction",
  "sqlx::sqlite::SqlitePoolOptions", "sqlx::sqlite::SqliteRow",  # one spelling short is a bypass
  "opencargo::storage::FilesystemStorage",  # step 2a — backstop only; the grep row enforces it
  "reqwest::Client" ]                       # step 8e: upstream + osv + oidc adapters only
disallowed-methods = ["sqlx::query", "sqlx::query_as", "sqlx::query_scalar", "sqlx::raw_sql"]  # step 8e; query_scalar has 31 sites outside src/db today (e.g. repositories.rs:197)
```

**`axum::Router` and `axum::extract::State` are deliberately absent.** HTTP is *meant* to be everywhere in the driving adapter; listing it would force the blanket allow described above and destroy the type check's value in exactly the files that need it. Domain purity is section 4.2's job, which can see what clippy cannot. With axum off the list, `#![allow(clippy::disallowed_types)]` in a file means exactly one thing: *this file is the persistence adapter*, and it appears in `src/adapters/sqlite/mod.rs` and `src/adapters/fs/mod.rs` and nowhere else. **`FilesystemStorage` is the entry that would break that rule, and it is also the entry clippy can barely enforce — so the grep is the real check and the lint is a backstop.** Two facts, both load-bearing. First, the path must be `opencargo::storage::FilesystemStorage`: clippy resolves a configured path's first segment against real crate names, `"crate"` is not special-cased, and clippy follows the `pub use filesystem::FilesystemStorage` at `storage/mod.rs:11`, so the `crate::`-spelled entry an earlier draft carried would have resolved to nothing and never fired once. Second, `disallowed_types` fires on `use` items, type positions and trait refs — **not in expression position**, so `FilesystemStorage::new(..)` at `server.rs:82`, `engine/fixture.rs:229` and `cleanup.rs:245` is invisible to it, and the item-level `#[allow]`s an earlier draft put on those three bindings were guarding nothing. Step 2a therefore drops those three allows, and 4.1's list of legal allows is exactly `src/adapters/sqlite/mod.rs` and `src/adapters/fs/mod.rs` at file level and nothing else — which restores the clean rule that a file-level allow means *this file is the persistence adapter*. The lint still catches the `Arc<FilesystemStorage>` field types and the `use` lines, which is worth one line of config; what catches the constructor is the grep row of 4.2, which is why that row is **kept and flipped to `== 0` at 2a rather than retired into clippy** — the same treatment `sqlx::query` and `SqlitePool` get, and the reason matters more here, because `s3.md` defers 2b by weeks and the type would otherwise be unguarded in between. One `cargo clippy` run on a deliberately-violating line is the check that the entry resolves at all. All 139 `sqlx` use sites work unchanged until the step that removes them; the file moves are never blocked on a lint.

### 4.2 The ratchet, which *is* step 0

`scripts/boundary.sh`, ~40 lines, run in CI. Each row is `n <= max`; the script fails when `n > max` and **prints a nag when `n < max`** so the maxes come down with the code. Counts below are measured at `6c9747a`, not estimated — a max set above the true count silently licenses new violations. **Every row counts occurrences, with `grep -roE`, and each `declare` line writes that unit down**, because occurrences and lines differ and the difference is a free violation: `\bdb::` is **232 occurrences over 231 lines**, so a max of 232 fed by a line count licenses one new call on a line that already has one. Rows that must ignore string literals **strip** them first (`sed 's/"[^"]*"//g'`) instead of dropping the whole line with `grep -v`, which hides a pool access sharing a line with a `.db"` literal. Re-measured under that rule the other maxima are unchanged: 219, 96, 72, 25, 16, 41.

- **`.db` must ignore string literals or its target is unreachable.** A bare `\.db\b` outside `src/db/` is 225, but 6 of those are filenames in strings, not pool accesses: `config.rs:117` (`"sqlite:./data/db/opencargo.db"`, **permanent**), `telemetry/cleanup.rs:234`, `proxy/engine/fixture.rs:208`, `auth/permissions.rs:84` (`test.db`), `registry/resolve.rs:320` (`t.db`), `policy/tests.rs:64` (`s.db`). - **The two SQL rows are scoped away from `src/db`, or step 0 is red on the day it lands.** In all of `src/` there are **170** `sqlx::query` sites and **148** `SqlitePool`/`Pool<Sqlite>` mentions; 96 and 72 are the counts *outside* `src/db/`. The predictable repair is to bump the maxima to 170/148, which silently licenses 74 new stray queries: exactly the failure this row exists to prevent. So both rows read `src/` minus `src/db` minus `src/adapters`, they start at 96 and 72, and they fall on their own as `src/db` shrinks into `src/adapters/sqlite` (both excluded, so a moved query is neither counted nor forgiven — the `db::` and `.db` rows are what track the move).

Word-boundary, max 25.

- **The first two rows are the ones that matter, and the earlier draft had neither.** `sqlx::query`, `SqlitePool` and `FilesystemStorage` between them cannot see `crate::db::f(&state.db, ..)`, which is 232 of the roughly 400 leak sites and the *only* form in 43 files (section 0). Section 6.2 already conceded that "232 `db::` call sites eventually change shape" while 5.1 guarded the pattern only in modules that do not exist yet. Over a two-month interleaved migration, `api/promote.rs` could go from 15 `db::` calls to 40 with CI green. These two rows close that, and they retire the whole exercise: the `db::` row reaches 0 exactly when `src/db` is deleted. Likewise **`cx.state` is 22 in `src/registry/`, 23 in `src/` with a trailing dot, and 25 as a word** — `policy/mod.rs:424` is the twenty-third dereference and it is the same bypass, so that row is unscoped.
- **A step's done-when names its caller files; a directory predicate is not a done-when.** Every port has callers in `src/api/`, and `src/api/` is not any step's directory. Written as "0 `sqlx` in `src/policy/`", "in `src/telemetry/vulns/`", "in `src/auth/`", "in `src/proxy/`", the done-whens all pass while five files hold ~25 `db::` occurrences and 4 raw queries between them: `api/permissions.rs` (8 + 1), `api/vulns.rs` (8 + 2), `api/deps.rs` (7 + 1), `api/policy.rs` (2), `proxy/purge.rs` (3 `db::`, of which `:38` is a live `get_repository_by_name(&state.db, ..)` — so step 5's `src/proxy/` predicate is satisfied with a pool call still in the directory, which is the blind metric section 0 warns about). Their ports all exist by 8b, so nothing is *missing*; what was missing is a row that forces them, leaving only 8e's blanket zero — and 8e is already the most overloaded step. So each store step's done-when is restated as **0 `db::` and 0 `sqlx::query` in that port's caller files, named in the step's files column**, and those five files are added to the columns of steps 6, 7, 8b (below). A target no step can reach is a target that gets loosened.
- **Without the `crate::adapters::` row the boundary's central rule is unenforced.** Every other row is keyed on the *old* spelling — `db::`, `.db`, `sqlx::query`, `SqlitePool`, `FilesystemStorage` — the two purity rows are scoped to `src/domain/` alone, and `clippy.toml` deliberately excludes axum and never mentions `crate::adapters` (4.1). So after step 8 a `use crate::adapters::sqlite::SqliteDashboardRead;` in `api/dashboard.rs` or in a use case scores 0 on every row and passes clippy. The row is 0 today (measured: zero occurrences in `src/`) and is **permanently** 0: `src/server.rs` is the composition root and the one legal importer, and it is the row's only written exclusion. **But it must match two spellings, because step 3 creates the second one.** `extern crate self as opencargo;` (7.2) exists so `opencargo::` resolves *inside* the lib — after which `use opencargo::adapters::sqlite::SqliteDashboardRead;` in `api/dashboard.rs` compiles, scores 0 on a `crate::`-anchored row and passes a `clippy.toml` that deliberately lists no `crate::adapters` entry (4.1). The domain-purity path row has the same hole: `opencargo::db::Repository` or `opencargo::server::AppState` inside `src/domain/` matches neither it nor the names row. The spelling is native to this tree, not hypothetical — `main.rs:7` already writes `use opencargo::{config, db, server};`. Hence `(crate|opencargo)::…` on both rows. **`src/main.rs` is the second half of the composition root and the exclusion list says so.** The binary is in `src/`, so it is in scope for every row, and it holds 2 `db::` occurrences and 1 `.db` — `main.rs:131-135`'s `Commands::Migrate` does `db::connect` then `db::migrate` with no `AppState` at all, and `:82` passes `app_state.db.clone()` to `start_cleanup_task`. **Raises are a diff, never a silent max bump.** A feature commit may legitimately need a row to go up — `s3.md`'s ledger and `pypi.md`'s file store both add rows that did not exist. What is forbidden is a count creeping up under a max that was set loose. **One shell-script hazard, stated because it will otherwise be got wrong:** `grep` exits 1 when it matches nothing, and under `set -euo pipefail` a row that has reached 0 will kill the script. Every count is `n=$(grep -rEc ... | awk '{s+=$1} END {print s+0}' || true)`, never a bare `grep | wc -l` under `set -e`. Still stronger than any grep or deny-list.

## 6. What it buys, what it costs, what not to abstract

### 6.1 Buys

- **Unit tests that do not touch a disk.** `auth/permissions.rs:82-120` builds a temp directory, a SQLite file, runs all 14 migrations and inserts fixture rows whose ids are hardcoded by AUTOINCREMENT order (`:76-80`) — to test a pure `match` on four booleans; **step 6** turns that into map literals. `registry/resolve.rs:313-346` builds a whole `AppState` plus a temp DB, then inserts malformed group rows with raw SQL because the seed refuses them, to test walk order; **step 7a** turns that into map literals, because the fixture exists to satisfy `Cx.state` — with step 5 as its precondition, since a `Cx.proxy` still holding a pool would keep the temp dir alive on its own (2.6).
- **Failure paths that are currently untestable.** `permissions.rs:39-44` maps a DB error to a 503 specifically so a failing database cannot silently grant access. No test covers it, because there is no way to make a real SQLite pool fail on demand. A fake returning `StoreError::Other` covers it in four lines — `Other`, because that is the injection that distinguishes the blanket collapse of 2.1 from a mapping table that only special-cases `Unavailable`.
- **A Postgres adapter becomes a scoped project** instead of "the largest single piece of work in this repository" (`ha-options.md` option 4): 15 traits against a contract suite, an `src/adapters/postgres/migrations/` directory beside 8a's SQLite one, and the FTS5 rewrite — which `SearchIndex` isolates to one read method whose index each adapter maintains itself (2.3) — and a second storage backend already proven, which is the same claim `s3.md` makes and step 2b delivers. **And the result is agent-legible:** 54 free functions over a pool give an AI agent no way to know what may call what; 15 named traits, a ratchet and a `clippy.toml` do, and the failure is a CI error rather than a review comment.

### 6.2 Costs

- **Churn.** 232 `db::` call sites, 225 `.db` field accesses and 25 `cx.state` mentions eventually change shape, spread over twelve steps. These are the two headline ratchet rows of 4.2, which is the point: the cost is measured and watched, not estimated once and forgotten.
- **Indirection.** `crate::db::get_repository_by_name(&state.db, name)` becomes `self.repos.by_name(name)`. One hop, and the hop is the point — but it is real and it makes a grep for "where is this query" a two-step grep. **Compile time** is the non-cost: 0.56 s lib and 0.93 s all-targets today, and `async_trait`'s `Box::pin` per method is single-digit percent, not a regime change.
- **The long-running branch.** The real risk, and the reason step 0 is a ratchet rather than a rewrite, step 9 is last, and 5.1 interleaves features. Rule: **no step lives more than a week on a branch.** If a step cannot be merged green in a week, split it — steps 7 and 8 are four commits each for exactly this reason, 8 having been a single week-sized row in an earlier draft while carrying 26 store methods, five infrastructure ports and the two worst files in the tree. **And fakes that lie** are the other cost: a fake store that only ever succeeds, or that does not cascade the way the schema does, proves less than a temp SQLite file. Section 7 is the answer and it is mandatory, not optional.

### 6.3 What NOT to abstract

- **axum.** No `WebFramework` port, and no `axum` entry in `clippy.toml` (4.1). Handlers, extractors and status codes *are* the driving adapter.
- **The HTTP client.** No `dyn HttpClient`. `tests/common/fake_upstream/` and `upstream_tap.rs` spawn real servers, which is the only honest way to test a proxy that must get conditional requests, `304`s, redirects (`proxy/mod.rs` redirect policy), ranges, bearer-token re-auth (`proxy/auth.rs`) and TLS right. A fake behind a trait would prove none of it and would silently pass when reqwest's behaviour changes.
- **tokio.** No `Spawner`, no `Sleep` port. `tokio::time::pause()` is the seam, and `PolicyEngine::unspawned` (`policy/mod.rs:324`) already shows the pattern: hand the caller the future instead of spawning it. **`Singleflight`** (`proxy/singleflight.rs`) and `TokenCache` (`proxy/auth.rs:124`) likewise: in-process coordination with one correct implementation; a trait adds a vtable and no option.
- **The filesystem below `StorageBackend`.** One port, not two. **Wire encodings** likewise: packument assembly, cargo index lines, OCI manifest media types, `maven-metadata.xml`, PyPI Simple HTML. Protocol, adapter, done. **The migrator**, until a second dialect exists (2.7). **Metrics.** `telemetry/metrics.rs:24-73` is already a facade over the `metrics` crate, which is itself a facade; a third would be ceremony, and `engine/mod.rs:160` calling `record_cache_hit` from near-domain code is acceptable (no state, no failure mode, no return value). **Config parsing** is serde over TOML and belongs to the binary. **Rate limiters**: `auth/rate_limit.rs` is in-process and correct for one instance; `ha-options.md` 1.4 shows they break at N instances — port them *when that trigger fires*, not before.
- **`AppState` itself.** Do not turn it into a bag of `Arc<dyn _>` that every use case receives. Each use case names the two or three ports it needs; `AppState` is the composition root's struct and stays in the adapter. `Cx` (2.6) is the one narrow exception, and it names eleven fields, not a state.

## 7. Test strategy

### 7.1 One `FakeDb`, not fifteen independent fakes

Fifteen separate `Mutex<HashMap<..>>` fakes cannot represent a cascade, and five of the eight coarse methods cascade across aggregates (2.4). In SQLite those cascades are half-free — `foreign_keys` is `ON` (`db/mod.rs:80-81`) and the FKs are declared — so the real adapter passes tests nobody wrote while hand-rolled fakes drift.

```rust
// tests/common/fakes.rs — all paths spelled `opencargo::…` (7.2)
pub struct FakeDb(Arc<Mutex<State>>);  // ONE state: repos, packages, versions, grants, tokens, cache rows, audit
impl FakeDb {  // 15 handles sharing one Arc: repos(), perms(), packages(), search(), …
    pub fn repos(&self) -> Arc<dyn RepositoryStore> { .. }  pub fn fail_next(&self, port: PortId, e: StoreError); }
```

A fake that cannot fail only proves the happy path, and the happy path is what the integration tests already cover — hence `fail_next`, which is what makes `permissions.rs:39-44`'s 503 testable at all.

### 7.2 Where the fakes live, because `feature = "fakes"` does not work here

`#[cfg(any(test, feature = "fakes"))]` does **not** make fakes visible to `tests/`: an integration test links the library built *without* `cfg(test)`, and Cargo has no way for a crate to enable its own feature for its own test targets. The two workarounds, and the choice:

- **Chosen:** the fakes live in `tests/common/fakes.rs`, compiled into every integration test that declares `mod common;`, and pulled into the lib **exactly once**, through a shim. No feature flag, no second build of the lib. **Rejected:** a self dev-dependency (`[dev-dependencies] opencargo = { path = ".", features = ["fakes"] }`). It works, it is the documented workaround, and it builds the library twice on every `cargo test`. If it is ever adopted, it must be written in `Cargo.toml` with a comment saying why, because it is not guessable.

**The chosen option needs two lines that are not obvious, and without them it does not compile.** Everything under `tests/common/` names the crate `opencargo::` (`tests/common/mod.rs:21-28`) and must, because an integration test is a separate crate. The same file compiled *inside* the lib by a `#[path]` include cannot resolve `opencargo::` at all in edition 2021; writing `crate::` instead breaks the integration build. And a raw `#[path = "../../tests/common/fakes.rs"]` string is depth-relative — `../../` from `src/auth/permissions.rs`, `../../../` from `src/registry/npm/leaves.rs` — so printing one form guarantees an agent copies the wrong one. Therefore, **in step 3** — and this alias is also why the adapter-import and domain-purity rows of 4.2 are spelled `(crate|opencargo)::` and not `crate::`: once `opencargo::` resolves inside the lib, it is a second, equally valid spelling of every path those rows police.
```rust
// src/lib.rs — one line, makes `opencargo::` resolve inside the lib too
extern crate self as opencargo;

// src/testing.rs — the ONLY file in the tree carrying a relative path to tests/
#[cfg(test)] #[path = "../tests/common/fakes.rs"] pub mod fakes;
#![allow(dead_code)]  // tests/common/fakes.rs — its own first line, never inherited
```
That last line is not decoration: `tests/common/mod.rs:1`'s `#![allow(dead_code)]` is an inner attribute on *that* module and does not reach `fakes.rs` once `src/testing.rs` includes it under a different parent, while `.github/workflows/ci.yml:64` runs `cargo clippy --all-targets --all-features -- -D warnings`, which compiles the lib's test target — so without it every uncalled fake helper is a denied warning and step 4 lands CI red.

`fakes.rs` writes `opencargo::…` throughout, like the rest of `tests/common/`. Unit tests say `use crate::testing::fakes::FakeDb;` and never `#[path]`. That is the whole trick, and it is written down because it is not guessable.

### 7.3 Two contract suites

**`store_contract!`, per port, instantiated against `FakeDb`, SQLite, and later Postgres.** The SQLite side is constructed through `SqliteStores::open(path)`, which returns the same handle set `FakeDb` exposes, so the contract module never names `SqlitePool` and the 41-site `tests/` row of 4.2 can still target 0 (4.2's declare line carries the exclusion in writing, for that module and nowhere else). It pins the behaviour the application relies on: `by_name` is case-sensitive but `get_package_nocase` (`db/mod.rs:228`) is not; `publish_version` is atomic and returns `Conflict` on a duplicate — **including when two `publish_version` calls for the same `package@version` run concurrently**, where the assertion is exactly one `Conflict` and zero `Other`, which is what catches an adapter that opened a deferred `BEGIN` (2.4); `entry(.., now)` reports `fresh: false` at exactly `expires_at == now`; **a `DateTime<Utc>` written through a store and read back equals the value written, both sides truncated to whole seconds** — a tolerance, so a precision-preserving adapter passes (1.5); the go `.info` regression is a *format* bug and is pinned by 1.5's RFC-3339 wire assertions, not by this clause; `SearchIndex::search(.., Some(q), ..)` returns `Package` rows **relevance-ordered**, not in a specific order — FTS5 `bm25` and Postgres `ts_rank` disagree, so the contract asserts *membership plus the top hit*, never a full ordering, while **`search(.., None, ..)` returns every package in scope up to `limit`** in no asserted order (the browse arm of 2.3, and the only assertion that can catch a browse silently becoming an empty result); **a row written with an explicit `now` reads back with that `now`, not the server clock** (1.5, which is what the `now` parameter of 2.2 exists for); and `SearchScope::PublicOnly` never returns a package from a private repository, which is `visibility_sql`'s predicate (`dashboard.rs:177-189`) turned into a port contract instead of a spliced SQL fragment. Writing that contract is how the FTS5 question (`ha-options.md` 1.1) stops being a surprise.

**`cascade_contract!`, cross-port, instantiated against the same adapters.** This is the suite a per-port macro structurally cannot contain, and it exists because the cascade lists in 2.4 are contract:

- `delete_empty` on a repository that still has a package ⇒ `Conflict` and the repository is **still there** (the assertion that keeps `tests/permissions_test.rs:595-603`'s 409 true once it moves off the handler); on an empty one ⇒ its grants, `proxy_cache_entries` and `proxy_cache_meta` rows are gone, and a second call is `NotFound`, not an error;
- delete a user ⇒ its tokens and grants are gone, its `audit_log` rows are **not** (`003:25` declares no cascade, 2.4), and a separate `PolicyStore::erase_user` removes its resolutions and verdicts while a homonymous other user's rows survive (`policy/store.rs:322-335`);
- publish a version ⇒ `SearchIndex::search` finds the package. Against SQLite this is only meaningful once 8a applies `007_fts5.sql` strictly and step 7's npm commit deletes the `LIKE` fallback at `npm/search.rs:129-140` — and only once `015_fts_rebuild.sql` has populated the index on an adopted install (2.3); until both land, the assertion passes through the fallback and proves nothing, and the contract file says so in a comment naming this line;
- delete a version ⇒ its `dist_tags`, `downloads` and `download_counts` rows are gone and no orphan remains (none of those four FKs cascades, `001:41,43,49` — so this is the assertion that keeps `telemetry/cleanup.rs:146-166`'s hand-rolled sequence honest once it is one transaction), and its blob was deleted **before** the transaction, per rule B;
- delete a manifest ⇒ its `oci_tags` and `oci_manifest_blobs` rows are gone and the returned `Orphaned.blob_digests` contains exactly the digests no manifest of that repository references any more — a blob another manifest still uses is absent from it (`oci/manifests.rs:287`); the file-level half is asserted on the use case, **after** the commit, not inside the store: the orphaned objects and the manifest object are gone from storage and the still-referenced blob is not;
- promote ⇒ the target blob exists **and** the target repository's package row exists **and** the version row exists **and** every dist-tag the source version held points at the new version — or none of the four, with no empty package row left behind in the target repo (2.4); with `fail_next` on the metadata transaction, the compensating `storage.delete(target)` of 2.4 runs and the target blob is **gone**, and the case that skips the compensation leaves a blob no row claims — a leak nothing reclaims until `StorageBackend::list` exists (2.4), asserted as such so that the day a sweeper is written this assertion is what changes.

### 7.4 The 41 raw-SQL sites in `tests/`

Nine integration files open their own pool and assert on tables: `tests/npm_proxy_test.rs`, `tests/cargo_proxy_test.rs`, `tests/go_proxy_test.rs`, `tests/oci_proxy_test.rs`, `tests/group_resolver_test.rs` (9 sites), `tests/vuln_test.rs`, `tests/cargo_e2e_test.rs`, `tests/docker_cli_e2e_test.rs`, `tests/common/mod.rs` (8). What they guarantee is **the SQLite schema**, not the ports. They are legitimate today and they are a lie the moment a second adapter exists: none of them would run against Postgres. The rule: they stay SQLite-only and the row is **ratchet-only** — it may not grow, and it comes down opportunistically as a step happens to replace one of them with a contract assertion or a port call. It is deliberately **not** targeted at 0: no step in section 5 owns that reduction, none is sized for it, and rule 7.5 #2 pushes the other way, so a target of 0 would be an unowned promise. **The Postgres gate therefore hangs on the contract suites instead, where it belongs: no Postgres adapter may be claimed to work until `store_contract!` and `cascade_contract!` pass, unmodified, against it** — which is the property those 41 sites were only ever standing in for. Write *that* in the README next to the ports claim.

### 7.5 Moves, stays, and the three rules

**Moves to unit tests** (from integration or temp-SQLite unit tests): the permission matrix (`permissions.rs:72+`), group walk order/dedup/depth (`resolve.rs:371-415`, 40 assertions currently behind a full `build_state` — step 7a), cache freshness and negative-entry TTL, policy report totals and the `max_id` delta (`policy/totals.rs`), dist-tag resolution, dependency and dependent graphs, vulnerability summarisation (`vulns/mod.rs` `summarize`), webhook selection (`db/mod.rs:1114`).

**Stays an integration test, with real clients, always:** all seven `*_proxy_test.rs` (conditional requests, stale-serving, singleflight, upstream auth), `docker_cli_e2e_test.rs` and `pnpm_e2e_test.rs` (real tool behaviour is the only oracle), `oci_auth_test.rs` (the bearer challenge), `tls_test.rs`, `ws_test.rs` (broadcast lag and `resync`), `cargo_e2e_test.rs`, `go_e2e_test.rs`, `e2e_scoped_test.rs`, and one end-to-end publish per format. **Any test asserting an HTTP status code stays an integration test** — that mapping is the adapter's contract with the world and no fake can check it.

**Keeping the 471 meaningful.** Three rules, and they are the difference between a refactor and a regression:

1. **Never delete a test in the commit that moves its subject.** Move the code, keep the integration test, add the unit test. Remove the integration test only in a later commit, only when a contract test covers it, and only if it asserts no status code.
2. **A step's integration tests must pass unmodified.** If `npm_test.rs` needs an edit to go green in step 7, the step changed behaviour and the edit is the bug report. The four format commits of step 7, and step 7a against `group_resolver_test.rs`, each stand or fall on this.
3. **The ratchet counts tests too.** `scripts/boundary.sh` asserts `unit >= 203` and `integration >= 268` minus an explicit, reviewed allowance per step. The suite may be rebalanced toward unit tests; it may not shrink quietly.

Expected shape at the end: ~350 unit tests (up from 203, most of them new failure-path, contract and cascade cases), ~250 integration (down from 268, only where a contract test genuinely subsumes one), and a suite that runs the interesting half without ever creating a temp directory.
